2Quick answer

On an income of $80,000 with a $50,000 deposit, $300 a month of other debts and a 5% mortgage over 30 years, the highest home price is $341,841.20. The rule is that the mortgage payment plus other debts stay under 28% of monthly income. That leaves a largest payment of $1,566.67 a month, supporting a loan of $291,841.20.

3The formula

Lenders often use the 28% rule: housing costs should stay under 28% of gross monthly income. The largest loan is found by running the loan formula backwards:

House affordability — in words: take 28% of monthly income, subtract other monthly debts, then work out the largest loan that payment supports and add the deposit. largest payment = (yearly income ÷ 12 × 28%) − monthly debts; largest price = largest loan + deposit

Formula source: the common 28% housing-cost rule, with the standard amortisation formula run backwards to find the loan. This is a planning guide only — a lender uses your credit history, job, debts and deposit, and may lend more or less than this figure.

4Worked example: $80,000 income, $50,000 deposit

  3. At 5% over 30 years, a payment of $1,566.67 supports a loan of $291,841.20.
  4. Answer: the highest home price is $291,841.20 + $50,000 = $341,841.20.

6Questions about house affordability

Why 28% of income?

28% is the common housing-cost rule lenders have used for years: the mortgage payment should stay under 28% of gross monthly income. It is a guide, not a law — some lenders allow more, and some buyers are safer with less.

Do my other debts matter?

Yes. Other monthly debts — car loans, cards, student loans — are taken off the 28% amount first, because that money is already spoken for. Higher debts mean a smaller largest payment and a lower home price.

What if my debts are higher than 28% of my income?

Then the largest payment is zero or less, and this calculator shows that no loan fits the 28% rule. Paying down debts or raising the deposit are the two ways to change that answer.

Does a bigger deposit raise the price I can afford?

Yes, dollar for dollar: the highest price is the largest loan plus the deposit. A $20,000 larger deposit raises the highest price by $20,000, and it also cuts the loan you need.

Does the mortgage term change the answer?

Yes. A longer term spreads the loan over more payments, so the same monthly payment supports a larger loan — but you pay much more interest over the whole term.

Is this a promise that a lender will lend this much?

No. This is an estimate for planning only. A lender checks your credit history, job, debts and deposit, and may lend more or less. Talk to a lender or a qualified adviser before you act.
