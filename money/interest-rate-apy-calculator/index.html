2Quick answer

The yearly yield, or APY, is the real yearly growth of a rate once compounding is counted. A nominal rate of 5% a year compounded monthly is really 5.12% a year, because each month's interest earns interest too. Type any nominal rate and compounding choice above and this calculator gives the APY instantly.

3The formula

A nominal rate leaves out the effect of compounding. The APY puts it back in, so two accounts that compound differently can be compared fairly:

Annual percentage yield — in words: divide the nominal rate by the number of times interest is added a year, add one, raise it to that number, and subtract one. APY = ((1 + r ÷ n)^n − 1) × 100

Formula source: the standard annual percentage yield formula. The nominal rate is typed by you — this site does not fetch live savings rates. This is an estimate only, not professional advice.

4Worked example: 5% nominal, compounded monthly

  1. Start with the figures: nominal yearly rate 5%, interest added 12 times a year.
  2. Work out the monthly rate: 5 ÷ 100 ÷ 12 = 0.004167.
  3. Put the numbers in the formula: ((1.004167)^12 − 1) × 100.
  4. Answer: the APY is 5.12%. So 100 grows to 105.12 in one year.

Try it in the tool above: type 5 with “Every month” picked, and the result line shows 5.12%.

6Questions about interest rate apy

What is the difference between a nominal rate and APY?

The nominal rate is the rate before compounding is counted; the APY is the real yearly growth after compounding. A 5% nominal rate compounded monthly is really 5.12% a year. When the rate compounds once a year, the two are the same.

Why does compounding more often raise the APY?

Because interest is added sooner, so it starts earning interest of its own sooner. At a 6% nominal rate, the APY is 6.00% compounded once a year, 6.09% twice a year, 6.14% four times a year and 6.17% monthly.

Which number should I compare when choosing an account?

Compare the APY, not the nominal rate. Two accounts can quote the same nominal rate but pay different APYs if one compounds monthly and the other once a year. The APY is the fair comparison.

Can the APY be lower than the nominal rate?

No. Compounding only ever adds to the growth, so the APY is always the nominal rate or higher. The two are equal only when interest is added once a year.

Does APY include tax or fees?

No. The figures are before any tax on interest and before any account fees. Your bank can tell you what tax and fees apply to your account.

How is the APY rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. For example, a 5% nominal rate compounded monthly gives an APY of 5.12%. The same rule is used in the answer, the bar and the table, so the figures always agree.
