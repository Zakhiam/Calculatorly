2Quick answer

The internal rate of return is the yearly rate that makes the present value of all the cash flows add up to zero. For example, investing $1,000.00 and receiving $300.00, $400.00 and $500.00 over three years earns 8.8963% a year. Type your own cash flows above, with the investment as a negative number.

3The formula

The IRR is the rate that balances the money going out against the money coming in, once each flow is discounted back to today. There is no plain formula for it, so the calculator finds it by narrowing down the rate step by step:

Internal rate of return — in words: the yearly rate r that makes the sum of each cash flow divided by one plus r to the power of its year equal zero. 0 = CF0 + CF1 ÷ (1 + r) + CF2 ÷ (1 + r)² + CF3 ÷ (1 + r)³ + …

Formula source: the standard net present value definition of IRR, solved by bisection (narrowing the rate until the net present value is zero). No rate is taken from anywhere else — only the cash flows you type.

4Worked example: -1000, 300, 400, 500

  1. Start with the cash flows: year 0: -1,000.00 (invested), year 1: 300.00, year 2: 400.00, year 3: 500.00.
  2. Test a rate of 8.00%: 300.00 ÷ 1.08 + 400.00 ÷ 1.08² + 500.00 ÷ 1.08³ = 277.78 + 342.94 + 396.92 = 1,017.64, which is above 1,000.00, so the true rate is higher.
  3. Test a rate of 9.00%: 300.00 ÷ 1.09 + 400.00 ÷ 1.09² + 500.00 ÷ 1.09³ = 275.23 + 336.68 + 386.09 = 998.00, which is below 1,000.00, so the true rate is between 8.00% and 9.00%.
  4. Narrowing the range step by step gives 8.8963%, where the discounted flows add up to exactly 1,000.00.
  5. Answer: the internal rate of return is 8.8963% a year.

6Questions about irr

What does the internal rate of return mean in plain words?

It is the yearly return an investment earns, once the timing of the money is taken into account. Money received early counts for more than money received late, because it can be used sooner. The IRR is the single yearly rate that makes all the flows balance out.

Why is the first cash flow a negative number?

Because it is money going out — the amount you invest. Later flows are money coming in, so they are positive. The calculator needs that minus sign to tell the investment apart from the returns. If the first flow is not negative, it shows a clear message.

What if the cash flows never change from negative to positive?

Then no internal rate of return exists, and the calculator says so plainly instead of giving a made-up number. For example, if every flow is positive, there is no investment to earn a return on; if every flow is negative, the money never comes back.

Can an investment have more than one IRR?

Yes, if the cash flows change sign more than once — for example, money goes out, comes in, then goes out again. In that case more than one rate can balance the flows, and this calculator gives the first one it finds. Most simple investments change sign only once, so they have one IRR.

How is IRR different from ROI?

ROI looks only at the total gain against the amount invested, and ignores when the money arrives. IRR takes the timing into account: money back early is worth more than money back late. The ROI Calculator on this site does the simple total-gain sum.
