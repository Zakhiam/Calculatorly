2Quick answer

A monthly mortgage payment comes from the loan amount, the yearly interest rate and the term. For example, a loan of 200,000 at 5% a year over 25 years costs about 1,169.18 a month. Type your own loan, rate and term above and this calculator gives the monthly payment, the total interest and the total paid.

3The formula

A mortgage is repaid in equal monthly payments that cover interest and part of the loan. The standard repayment formula is:

Monthly mortgage payment — in words: take the monthly interest rate, multiply it by the loan, and divide by one minus the monthly rate factor raised to the number of months. M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where r is the monthly rate and n is the number of months

Formula source: the standard amortisation formula for a fixed-rate repayment loan. The interest rate is typed by you — this site does not fetch live mortgage rates. This is an estimate only, not professional advice.

4Worked example: 200,000 at 5% over 25 years

  1. Start with the loan, rate and term: loan 200,000, yearly rate 5%, term 25 years (300 months).
  2. Work out the monthly rate: 5 ÷ 100 ÷ 12 = 0.004167.
  3. Put the numbers in the formula: 200,000 × 0.004167 ÷ (1 − (1.004167)^−300).
  4. Answer: the monthly payment is £1,169.18. Over 300 months that is £350,754.02 in total, of which £150,754.02 is interest.

Try it in the tool above: type 200000, 5 and 25 with United Kingdom picked, and the result line shows £1,169.18. Change the country to see the same payment in dollars.

6Questions about mortgage

What does the monthly payment include?

Only the loan repayment and the interest on it. It does not include property tax, home insurance, mortgage insurance or lender fees, which are charged separately in most countries.

What happens if the interest rate changes?

The payment changes with it. A higher rate means a higher monthly payment and more total interest; a lower rate means the opposite. Type the new rate above to see the new payment at once.

Does a longer term mean a lower payment?

Yes, the monthly payment falls, because the loan is spread over more months — but the total interest rises, because you pay interest for longer. Compare 25 years with 30 years above to see both effects.

Why is the total interest so large?

Because interest is charged on the amount still owed every month, and a mortgage runs for decades. On a 200,000 loan at 5% over 25 years, the interest is about 150,755 — roughly three quarters of the loan again.

How are the answers rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. Each line is rounded on its own for display, so multiplying the rounded monthly payment by the number of months can differ by a few cents from the total shown.

Is this the payment my bank will charge?

Not exactly. Banks round payments their own way, add fees, and may use a different day count. Use this figure to plan and compare; ask your lender for the exact payment before you sign anything.
