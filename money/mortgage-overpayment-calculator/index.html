2Quick answer

Paying a little extra each month cuts the interest and shortens the loan. On a $250,000 loan at 5% over 25 years, paying $100 extra a month saves $25,381.91 in interest and ends the loan 35 months early. Type your loan and extra payment above to see your saving.

3The formula

Each monthly payment is worked out on the balance left, so an extra payment cuts the balance and every later interest charge with it. The loan ends when the balance reaches zero.

Interest saved by overpaying — in words: work out the total interest with the normal payment only, work out the total interest with the extra payment added, and take the second away from the first. interest saved = total interest without extra payments − total interest with extra payments

Formula source: the standard loan payment formula with monthly amortisation, on the four numbers you type. The rate is the one you type, and it is assumed to stay the same for the whole term. No live rate is used.

4Worked example: $250,000 at 5% over 25 years, $100 extra a month

  1. Normal monthly payment: $1,461.48. Total interest over 300 months: $188,442.53.
  2. With $100 extra a month the payment is $1,561.48, and the loan ends after 265 months.
  3. Total interest with the extra payments: $163,060.62.
  4. Answer: $188,442.53 − $163,060.62 = $25,381.91 saved, and the loan ends 35 months early.

Try it in the tool above: type 250000, 5, 25 and 100, and the result line shows $25,381.91.

6Questions about mortgage overpayment

Why does a small extra payment save so much?

Because it cuts the balance early, and every later month charges interest on that smaller balance. The saving grows over the years, so the same $100 a month saves more on a long loan than on a short one.

Is there a limit on extra payments?

Many lenders allow 10% of the balance a year without a charge, and charge for more. This page does not check your lender's limit — ask them before paying a large extra amount.

What if the interest rate changes?

Then the saving changes too. This page holds the rate you type for the whole term. If your rate rises, the saving from overpaying grows, because more interest is avoided.

Should I overpay or save the money instead?

Compare the loan rate with the savings rate. If the loan rate is higher, overpaying usually wins. Use the Savings vs Loan Payoff Calculator on this site to compare the two choices with your own rates.

Does the extra payment shorten the term or cut the monthly payment?

This page shortens the term: the monthly payment stays the same and the loan ends early. Some lenders cut the monthly payment instead, which saves less interest.

Is this calculator free, and do I need to sign up?

Yes, it is free, and no sign-up is needed. Nothing is uploaded: the calculation runs in your browser on your own device, and the numbers you type are not sent anywhere.
