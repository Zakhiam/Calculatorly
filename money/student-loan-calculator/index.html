2Quick answer

A student loan payment uses the fixed-rate loan formula: the monthly rate is the yearly rate divided by 12, and the payment is the loan amount times that rate, divided by one minus the discount factor over the term. For example, a $30,000.00 loan at 5.00% a year over 10 years costs $318.20 a month. Type your own loan above.

3The formula

A student loan is a fixed-rate loan: the same payment every month, with part of each payment covering interest and the rest paying down the loan:

Monthly student loan payment — in words: divide the yearly rate by 12 to get the monthly rate, then divide the loan amount times that rate by one minus the monthly rate factor over all the months. payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^(−n)), where r is the monthly rate and n is the number of months

Formula source: the standard fixed-rate loan (amortisation) formula. No live rate is used — the interest rate is the one you type.

4Worked example: $30,000.00 at 5.00% over 10 years

  1. Start with the loan: $30,000.00, a yearly rate of 5.00%, a term of 10 years.
  2. Find the monthly rate: 5.00 ÷ 100 ÷ 12 = 0.0041667, and the number of months: 10 × 12 = 120.
  3. Apply the formula: $30,000.00 × 0.0041667 ÷ (1 − (1.0041667)^(−120)) = $318.20.
  4. Total paid over the term: $318.20 × 120 = $38,183.59, so the total interest is $38,183.59 − $30,000.00 = $8,183.59.
  5. Answer: the monthly payment is $318.20.

Try it in the tool above: type 30000, 5 and 10, and the result line shows $318.20.

6Questions about student loan

How is a student loan payment worked out?

With the fixed-rate loan formula: the yearly rate is divided by 12 to get the monthly rate, and the payment is the loan amount times that rate, divided by one minus the discount factor over the number of months. This calculator does that sum instantly, and shows the total paid and the total interest in the table below.

What if my interest rate is zero?

Then the payment is simply the loan amount divided by the number of months. For example, a $10,000.00 loan over 5 years with no interest costs $10,000.00 ÷ 60 = $166.67 a month, and the total interest is $0.00.

Does a longer loan term always cost more in total?

Yes, if the rate stays the same. A longer term gives a smaller monthly payment, because the loan is spread over more months, but more interest builds up over that extra time, so the total paid is higher. The table under the result shows both figures, so you can compare terms.

Can I use this for any fixed-rate loan, not just a student loan?

Yes. The maths is the same for any loan with a fixed rate and equal monthly payments, such as a personal loan. It does not cover loans with a variable rate, or loans where the payment changes, because those need the rate at each step.

What limits does this calculator have?

The loan term must be between 1 and 50 years and the yearly rate between 0% and 100%. Amounts up to 1,000,000,000,000 are accepted. An empty box, a term outside the range, or a number above the limit shows a clear message instead of a result.

Is this calculator free, and do I need to sign up?

Yes, it is free, and no sign-up is needed. Nothing is uploaded: the calculation runs in your browser on your own device, and the numbers you type are not sent anywhere.
