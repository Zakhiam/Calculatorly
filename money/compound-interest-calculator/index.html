2Quick answer

With compound interest, each interest payment itself earns interest, so savings grow faster the longer they are left. For example, 1,000 at 5% a year compounded monthly grows to about 1,647.01 in 10 years. Type your own starting amount, rate and years above and this calculator gives the future value and the interest earned.

3The formula

Interest is added on top of interest, so the amount grows by a growing amount each time. The formula is:

Compound interest — in words: divide the yearly rate by the number of times interest is added a year, add one, raise it to the total number of additions, and multiply by the starting amount. future value = P × (1 + r ÷ n)^(n × t)

Formula source: the standard compound interest formula. The interest rate is typed by you — this site does not fetch live savings rates, and no tax is taken off here. This is an estimate only, not professional advice.

4Worked example: 1,000 at 5% for 10 years, monthly

  1. Start with the figures: starting amount 1,000, yearly rate 5%, 10 years, interest added 12 times a year.
  2. Work out the monthly rate: 5 ÷ 100 ÷ 12 = 0.004167, and the number of additions: 12 × 10 = 120.
  3. Put the numbers in the formula: 1,000 × (1.004167)^120.
  4. Answer: the future value is 1,647.01, so the interest earned is 647.01.

Try it in the tool above: type 1000, 5 and 10 with “Every month” picked, and the result line shows 1,647.01.

6Questions about compound interest

What is the difference between compound and simple interest?

Simple interest is paid only on the starting amount, so it grows by the same amount every year. Compound interest is paid on the starting amount plus all the interest earned so far, so it grows by more each year. Over 10 years the difference is large.

Does compounding more often make a big difference?

A small one. At 5% a year, 1,000 over 10 years grows to 1,628.89 compounded once a year and 1,647.01 compounded monthly — about 18 more. The rate and the time matter far more than how often interest is added.

What is the effective yearly rate?

It is the real yearly growth once compounding is counted: at a nominal 5% compounded monthly, the effective rate is 5.12% a year. It is the number to compare when two accounts compound differently.

Is tax taken off the interest here?

No. The figures are before any tax on interest, and before any fees or withdrawals. Your bank or tax authority can tell you what tax applies to your interest.

What if the interest rate is zero?

The money does not grow at all: the future value is exactly the starting amount, and the interest earned is 0.00. The calculator shows that as a real answer.

How are the answers rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. The same rule is used in the answer, the bar and the table, so the figures always agree.
