2Quick answer

An amount of $100 from 10 years ago is worth $134.39 in today's money at a yearly inflation rate of 3% that you enter yourself. Prices rise a little each year, so the same basket of goods costs more now. This site does not fetch any live inflation rate — the rate you type is your own assumption.

3The formula

Inflation compounds, like interest in reverse: each year's rise builds on the last year's higher price:

Inflation — in words: take the past amount and grow it by the yearly inflation rate, once for each year, compounding year after year. today's amount = past amount × (1 + yearly rate ÷ 100)^years

Formula source: the standard compound growth formula. The inflation rate is an assumption you type — this site holds no inflation data and fetches no live rate. For a real figure, use the official inflation rate for the country and years you mean.

4Worked example: $100 from 10 years ago at 3% a year

  1. Start with the numbers: amount $100, years 10, yearly inflation rate 3% (your own assumption).
  2. The yearly factor is 1 + 3 ÷ 100 = 1.03.
  3. Raise it to 10 years: 1.03^10 = 1.3439, then multiply: $100 × 1.3439 = $134.39.
  4. Answer: $100 from 10 years ago buys what $134.39 buys today — a rise of $34.39, or 34.39%.

Try it in the tool above: type 100, 10 and 3, and the result line shows $134.39.

6Questions about inflation

Where does the inflation rate come from?

From you. This site does not fetch any live inflation rate, so you type the yearly rate you want to assume. For a real check, look up the official inflation rate for your country and years — for example the national statistics office.

Why does inflation compound?

Because each year's price rise applies to the new, higher price, not to the old one. At 3% a year, prices rise 3% in year one, then 3% of the higher price in year two, and so on. That is why 10 years at 3% gives 34.39%, not 30%.

What is the difference between inflation and the cost of living?

Inflation is the general rise in prices over time. The cost of living is what you actually pay, which depends on what you buy. Your own costs can rise faster or slower than the general rate.

Can the inflation rate be zero or negative?

Yes. A 0% rate means prices stay the same, so $100 stays $100. A negative rate means prices fall, so the amount in today's money is smaller than the past amount.

How many years can I count?

This calculator counts up to 100 years. Beyond that the numbers stop meaning much, because no rate stays steady for that long.

Is this financial advice?

No. This is an estimate for planning, from the numbers you type, with a rate you assume. It is not a forecast of real prices.
