2Quick answer

To add VAT, multiply the price by the VAT rate and add it on: 20% VAT on 100 is 20, so the price with VAT is 120. To take VAT out of a price that already includes it, divide by one plus the rate. Pick the direction above for the net price, the VAT and the gross price instantly.

3The formula

VAT is a percent added to a net price. The direction matters: adding VAT and removing VAT are different sums, so pick the right one first:

VAT — in words: to add VAT, take the rate percent of the net price and add it on; to remove VAT, divide the gross price by one plus the rate. VAT = net × rate ÷ 100, and gross = net + VAT; net = gross ÷ (1 + rate ÷ 100)

Formula source: the standard arithmetic of value added tax. The VAT rate is typed by you, because rates differ by country and by item — this site does not guess your rate. Amounts are in your own currency.

4Worked example: add 20% VAT to 100

  1. Start with the net price and the rate: price 100, VAT rate 20%, adding VAT.
  2. Work out the VAT: 100 × 20 ÷ 100 = 20.
  3. Add the VAT to the net price: 100 + 20 = 120.
  4. Answer: the price with VAT is 120.00, and the VAT is 20.00.

Try it in the tool above: type 100 and 20 with “Price without VAT” picked, and the result line shows 120.00. Switch to “Price with VAT” and type 120 to see the VAT taken back out.

6Questions about vat

How do I add VAT to a price?

Multiply the price by the VAT rate and divide by 100, then add that VAT to the price. At 20% on 100, the VAT is 20 and the price with VAT is 120. Pick “Price without VAT” above and the calculator does it for you.

How do I take VAT out of a price?

Divide the price by one plus the rate as a share. At 20% VAT, divide by 1.20: a price of 120 with VAT in it is 120 ÷ 1.20 = 100 without VAT, so the VAT inside is 20. Pick “Price with VAT” above.

Why is the VAT inside 120 not 24?

Because 20% VAT is worked out on the net price of 100, not on the gross price of 120. The VAT inside a price that already includes 20% VAT is one sixth of it: 120 ÷ 6 = 20. Taking 20% off the gross price is a common mistake.

What VAT rate should I type?

Type the rate that applies to your item in your country — for example 20% is the standard rate in the UK, but food, books and other items can carry a lower rate. This site does not know your item or your country, so it never guesses a rate for you.

How are the answers rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. For example, taking 20% VAT out of 99 gives a net price of 82.50. The same rule is used in the answer, the bar and the table, so the figures always agree.

Is the VAT shown here what I pay at the till?

Only if the price and rate you typed are the ones that apply. The tool does the arithmetic exactly; whether the rate is right for your item is something to check against the seller or your tax authority.
