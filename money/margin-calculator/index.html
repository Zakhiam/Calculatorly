2Quick answer

Margin and markup both come from the same profit, but they divide it by different numbers: margin divides by the selling price, markup by the cost. For example, a cost of $60.00 and a price of $90.00 give a profit of $30.00, a margin of 33.33% and a markup of 50.00%. Type your own figures above.

3The formula

The profit is the same in both sums — only the number it is measured against changes. That is why margin and markup are different numbers for the same sale:

Margin and markup — in words: margin is the profit divided by the selling price, times 100; markup is the profit divided by the cost, times 100. Profit is the selling price minus the cost. margin = ((price − cost) ÷ price) × 100; markup = ((price − cost) ÷ cost) × 100

Formula source: the standard definitions of gross margin and markup. No rate or price is taken from anywhere else — only the cost and the selling price you type.

4Worked example: cost $60.00, price $90.00

  1. Start with the figures: cost $60.00, selling price $90.00.
  2. Find the profit: $90.00 − $60.00 = $30.00.
  3. Work out the margin: $30.00 ÷ $90.00 × 100 = 33.33%. The margin is measured against the price.
  4. Work out the markup: $30.00 ÷ $60.00 × 100 = 50.00%. The markup is measured against the cost.
  5. Answer: the margin is 33.33% and the markup is 50.00%.

6Questions about margin

What is the difference between margin and markup?

Margin divides the profit by the selling price; markup divides the same profit by the cost. For a cost of $60.00 and a price of $90.00, the profit is $30.00, the margin is $30.00 ÷ $90.00 = 33.33% and the markup is $30.00 ÷ $60.00 = 50.00%. Margin is always smaller than markup for the same sale.

Which one should I use for pricing?

Use margin when you want to know what share of each sale you keep, and markup when you want to set a price from a cost. For example, a 50.00% markup on a $60.00 cost gives a $90.00 price. Many sellers mix them up and under-price, so this page always shows both, clearly labelled.

Can the margin be more than 100%?

No. The margin is the profit as a share of the price, and the profit can never be more than the price itself. A margin of 100.00% means the cost was zero. The markup has no such cap: it can be well over 100%.

What if the selling price is lower than the cost?

Then the profit is negative, and so are the margin and the markup — the sale loses money. For example, a cost of $100.00 and a price of $80.00 give a profit of −$20.00, a margin of −25.00% and a markup of −20.00%. A negative result is a real answer, not an error.

What if the cost is zero?

The margin is still a real answer — 100.00%, because the whole price is profit. The markup is not defined, because it would divide by zero, so the table shows that plainly instead of a made-up number.
