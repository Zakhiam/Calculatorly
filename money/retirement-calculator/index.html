2Quick answer

A retirement pot grows from three things: what you already have, what you add each month, and the growth on both. For example, 10,000 saved now plus 200 a month at 6% a year could be worth about 261,129 in 30 years. Type your figures above and this calculator gives the projected pot, with the growth rate as your assumption.

3The formula

Money grows month by month, and each monthly saving starts growing from the month it is paid in. The formula is:

Retirement projection — in words: grow the current savings by the monthly growth rate for every month, and add the monthly savings grown the same way. pot = current × (1 + r)^n + monthly × ((1 + r)^n − 1) ÷ r

Formula source: the standard future value formula with monthly contributions at the end of each month. The growth rate is typed by you as your own assumption — it is not a promise, and no fees, tax or inflation are taken off here. This is an estimate only, not professional advice.

4Worked example: 10,000 now, 200 a month, 6% for 30 years

  1. Start with the figures: current savings 10,000, monthly saving 200, yearly growth 6%, 30 years (360 months).
  2. Work out the monthly growth rate: 6 ÷ 100 ÷ 12 = 0.005, and the growth factor over 360 months: (1.005)^360 = 6.0226.
  3. Put the numbers in the formula: 10,000 × 6.0226 + 200 × (6.0226 − 1) ÷ 0.005.
  4. Answer: the projected pot is 261,128.76. You pay in 82,000.00 and the growth adds 179,128.76.

Try it in the tool above: type 10000, 200, 6 and 30, and the result line shows 261,128.75.

6Questions about retirement

Is the projected pot guaranteed?

No. The growth rate is your own assumption, and real investment growth goes up and down. The figure shows what would happen if growth stayed steady at the rate you typed — it is a plan, not a promise.

What growth rate should I type?

Type a rate you think is fair for your savings, and try a lower one too, to see how much the answer moves. Many people try 4%, 6% and 8% side by side. This site does not suggest a rate for you.

Does the figure include inflation?

No. The pot is shown in today's money terms only if you type a growth rate that is already after inflation. If your rate is before inflation, the pot will buy less than the number suggests. Try a lower rate to allow for inflation.

Are fees or tax taken off here?

No. Investment fees, tax on growth and any pension charges are not taken off. They all reduce the real pot, so ask your provider what they charge.

What if I save nothing each month?

The pot is just your current savings grown by the rate you typed. For example, 50,000 at 4% a year for 10 years grows to about 74,541 on its own.

How are the answers rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. The same rule is used in the answer, the bar and the table, so the figures always agree.
