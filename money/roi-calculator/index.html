2Quick answer

Return on investment is the gain divided by the amount invested, multiplied by 100. For example, investing $1,000.00 and receiving $1,250.00 is a gain of $250.00, and $250.00 ÷ $1,000.00 × 100 = 25.00%. Type the amount invested and the amount received above.

3The formula

ROI measures the gain against the money that was put in, as a percent. A positive result is a gain; a negative result is a loss:

Return on investment — in words: subtract the amount invested from the amount received, divide the gain by the amount invested, then multiply by 100. ROI = ((amount received − amount invested) ÷ amount invested) × 100

Formula source: the standard definition of return on investment. No rate or price is taken from anywhere else — only the two amounts you type.

4Worked example: invest $1,000.00, receive $1,250.00

  1. Start with the two amounts: invested $1,000.00, received $1,250.00.
  2. Find the gain: $1,250.00 − $1,000.00 = $250.00.
  3. Divide the gain by the amount invested: $250.00 ÷ $1,000.00 = 0.25.
  4. Multiply by 100: 0.25 × 100 = 25.00 — the result is positive, so it is a gain.
  5. Answer: the return on investment is 25.00%.

6Questions about roi

How do I work out ROI by hand?

Subtract the amount invested from the amount received to get the gain, divide the gain by the amount invested, then multiply by 100. For example, $1,250.00 received from a $1,000.00 investment is a $250.00 gain, and $250.00 ÷ $1,000.00 × 100 = 25.00%.

What does a negative ROI mean?

It means the investment lost money. For example, investing $2,000.00 and receiving $1,800.00 is a loss of $200.00, and −$200.00 ÷ $2,000.00 × 100 = −10.00%. A negative result is a real answer, not an error.

Can ROI be more than 100%?

Yes. If the amount received is more than double the amount invested, the gain is bigger than the investment itself. For example, investing $500.00 and receiving $1,500.00 is a gain of $1,000.00, which is 200.00% ROI.

How is ROI different from IRR?

ROI looks only at the total gain against the amount invested, and ignores when the money arrives. IRR takes the timing into account: money back early is worth more than money back late. The IRR Calculator on this site does the timing-aware sum.

What if the amount invested is zero?

The return cannot be worked out from an investment of zero, because the formula divides by the amount invested and dividing by zero has no answer. The calculator shows a clear message instead of a made-up number.
