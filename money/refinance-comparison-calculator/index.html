2Quick answer

Refinancing a $200,000 balance from 7% to 5% over 30 years saves $256.96 a month. The old payment is $1,330.60 and the new payment is $1,073.64. The $4,000 fees pay for themselves in 16 months, because $4,000 divided by $256.96 is 15.6 months, rounded up. After that month, the saving is yours to keep.

3The formula

Each payment uses the standard loan formula on the same balance and term, once at the old rate and once at the new rate:

Refinance saving — in words: work out the monthly payment at the old rate and at the new rate, then subtract; divide the fees by the monthly saving for the break-even month. monthly saving = old payment − new payment; break-even months = fees ÷ monthly saving, rounded up

Formula source: the standard amortisation formula for a fixed-rate loan. No rate is fetched from anywhere — you type both rates, and the page uses only the five numbers you type.

4Worked example: $200,000 from 7% to 5%

  1. Start with the numbers: balance $200,000, current rate 7%, new rate 5%, term 30 years, fees $4,000.
  2. The old payment at 7% over 30 years is $1,330.60 a month. The new payment at 5% is $1,073.64 a month.
  3. The monthly saving is $1,330.60 − $1,073.64 = $256.96.
  4. Answer: you save $256.96 a month, and the fees pay for themselves in 16 months ($4,000 ÷ $256.96 = 15.6, rounded up).

6Questions about refinance comparison

What is the break-even month?

It is the month when the savings so far have covered the refinancing fees. With $4,000 of fees and $256.96 saved a month, that is month 16. Before that month you are still out of pocket; after it, every month saves you money.

What if the new rate is higher than the old rate?

Then refinancing costs more each month, and there is no break-even month — the fees never pay for themselves. The calculator says so plainly instead of showing a saving that does not exist.

Does a shorter new term change the answer?

Yes. A shorter term means a higher monthly payment, so the monthly saving shrinks or turns into an extra cost — but the loan ends years sooner and the total interest falls a lot. Compare the total saving over the term in the table, not just the monthly figure.

Are the fees added to the loan?

In this calculator the fees are paid on their own and counted in the break-even month. In real life some lenders add the fees to the loan balance, which raises the new payment and pushes the break-even month later.

Should I refinance for a small saving?

Only if you will stay in the home past the break-even month. If you move or refinance again before then, the fees cost more than you saved.

Is this financial advice?

No. This is an estimate for planning, from the numbers you type. Refinancing has tax and legal effects that differ by country, so check with a qualified adviser before you act.
