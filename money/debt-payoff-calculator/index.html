2Quick answer

With $1,000 a month to pay three debts of $3,000 at 20%, $5,000 at 10% and $2,000 at 15%, snowball clears them in 11 months with $614.09 of interest, while avalanche also takes 11 months but costs $584.66 of interest. Snowball pays the smallest debt first; avalanche pays the highest rate first.

3The formula

Both methods use the same monthly count. Only the order changes. The count runs month by month until every balance reaches zero:

Debt payoff — in words: each month add one month of interest to every debt, then pay the debts in the chosen order until the monthly payment is used up, and repeat until all debts reach zero. each month: balance grows by balance × yearly rate ÷ 12, then the payment is used up in order — smallest balance first (snowball) or highest rate first (avalanche)

Formula source: the standard month-by-month loan payoff count, run twice — once in snowball order and once in avalanche order. No rate is fetched from anywhere — you type your own rates, and the page uses only the numbers you type. The count leaves out minimum payments and fees.

4Worked example: three debts, $1,000 a month

  1. Start with the numbers: $1,000 a month to pay debts of $3,000 at 20%, $5,000 at 10% and $2,000 at 15%.
  2. Snowball order is smallest first: the $2,000 debt, then the $3,000 debt, then the $5,000 debt. Avalanche order is highest rate first: the $3,000 debt at 20%, then the $2,000 debt at 15%, then the $5,000 debt at 10%.
  3. Each month, interest is added to every debt, then the $1,000 payment is used up in order, clearing one debt before moving to the next.
  4. Answer: both orders take 11 months, but snowball costs $614.09 in interest and avalanche costs $584.66 — avalanche saves $29.43 here.

Try it in the tool above: type 1000, then 3000 at 20, 5000 at 10 and 2000 at 15, and the result line shows both orders.

6Questions about debt payoff

What is the snowball method?

Snowball pays the smallest debt first, whatever its rate. Clearing a whole debt quickly feels like a win and frees up money for the next debt. It can cost more interest than avalanche, but many people stick with it longer.

What is the avalanche method?

Avalanche pays the highest-rate debt first, whatever its size. That cuts the most interest, so it is usually the cheapest way to clear all the debts. In the example above it saves $29.43 against snowball.

Which method should I choose?

Avalanche costs less interest in almost every case. Snowball wins only if quick wins keep you going when a big high-rate debt would feel endless. Pick the one you will actually stick with — a plan you drop costs more than either method.

How do I use this for more than three debts?

Add the smallest debts together, or run the calculator twice: first for the three debts you will clear first, then again for the rest with the payment you will have after those are gone.

What if my payment does not cover the interest?

Then the debts never fall — they grow. The calculator says so plainly instead of showing a payoff date that does not exist. Raise the payment above the first month's interest, or the count has no end.

Is this financial advice?

No. This is an estimate for planning, from the numbers you type. It leaves out minimum payments, fees and rate changes, so check your own statements before you act.
