2Quick answer

A monthly loan payment comes from the amount borrowed, the yearly interest rate and the term. For example, a loan of 10,000 at 7% a year over 5 years costs about 198.01 a month. Type your own loan, rate and term above and this calculator gives the monthly payment, the total interest and the total paid, in your own currency.

3The formula

A loan is repaid in equal monthly payments that cover interest and part of the loan. The standard repayment formula is:

Monthly loan payment — in words: take the monthly interest rate, multiply it by the loan, and divide by one minus the monthly rate factor raised to the number of months. M = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where r is the monthly rate and n is the number of months

Formula source: the standard amortisation formula for a fixed-rate loan. The interest rate is typed by you — this site does not fetch live loan rates. This is an estimate only, not professional advice.

4Worked example: 10,000 at 7% over 5 years

  1. Start with the loan, rate and term: loan 10,000, yearly rate 7%, term 5 years (60 months).
  2. Work out the monthly rate: 7 ÷ 100 ÷ 12 = 0.005833.
  3. Put the numbers in the formula: 10,000 × 0.005833 ÷ (1 − (1.005833)^−60).
  4. Answer: the monthly payment is 198.01. Over 60 months that is 11,880.72 in total, of which 1,880.72 is interest.

Try it in the tool above: type 10000, 7 and 5, and the result line shows 198.01.

6Questions about loan

What does the monthly payment include?

Only the loan repayment and the interest on it. It does not include fees, insurance or any extra charges the lender adds, which are charged separately.

Does a longer term mean a lower payment?

Yes, the monthly payment falls, because the loan is spread over more months — but the total interest rises, because you pay interest for longer. Compare 5 years with 7 years above to see both effects.

What happens if the interest rate is zero?

The payment is simply the loan divided by the number of months: a 5,000 loan over 2 years at 0% is 208.33 a month, with no interest at all.

How is the total interest worked out?

Multiply the monthly payment by the number of months to get the total paid, then subtract the amount borrowed. The difference is the total interest.

How are the answers rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. Each line is rounded on its own for display, so multiplying the rounded monthly payment by the number of months can differ by a few cents from the total shown.

Is this the payment my lender will charge?

Not exactly. Lenders round payments their own way and add fees, and may use a different day count. Use this figure to plan and compare; ask your lender for the exact payment before you sign anything.
