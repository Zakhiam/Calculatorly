2Quick answer

A $5,000 card balance at 20% a year, paid off at $200 a month, takes 33 months to clear and costs $1,522.10 in interest. Each month interest is added to the balance first, then your payment cuts it down. The last payment is smaller than $200, because only a small balance is left by then.

3The formula

The payoff time comes from the same sum the table counts month by month: interest is added, then the payment is taken off, until nothing is left:

Credit card payoff — in words: each month add one month of interest to the balance, then take off the payment, and repeat until the balance reaches zero. months = −ln(1 − balance × monthly rate ÷ payment) ÷ ln(1 + monthly rate); the table counts month by month for the exact interest

Formula source: the standard loan payoff formula, checked month by month in the table on this page. No rate is fetched from anywhere — you type your own rate, and the page uses only the three numbers you type.

4Worked example: $5,000 at 20%, paying $200 a month

  1. Start with the numbers: balance $5,000, yearly rate 20% (1.6667% a month), payment $200 a month.
  2. In month 1, interest is $5,000 × 1.6667% = $83.33. After the $200 payment the balance is $5,000 + $83.33 − $200 = $4,883.33.
  3. The same step repeats each month. The balance falls faster as the interest shrinks, and the last payment is smaller than $200.
  4. Answer: the card is clear in 33 months, and the total interest paid is $1,522.10.

Try it in the tool above: type 5000, 20 and 200, and the result line shows 33 months.

6Questions about credit card payoff

Why does it take longer than the balance divided by the payment?

Because interest is added every month. $5,000 divided by $200 is 25 months, but the interest adds $1,522.10 along the way, so the real time is 33 months. Only a 0% rate would give 25 months.

What if my payment does not cover the monthly interest?

Then the balance never falls — it grows. For example, a $50 payment on a $5,000 balance at 20% cannot cover the $83.33 of interest in the first month. The calculator says so plainly instead of showing a payoff time that does not exist.

Does paying more each month help a lot?

Yes, more than most people expect. A higher payment cuts the balance faster, so less interest builds up, and the card is clear much sooner. Try a larger payment in the tool above and watch the months and the interest fall.

Why is the last payment smaller?

Because the balance left in the last month is smaller than the usual payment. You only pay what is left, plus that month's interest — never a full $200 for a balance of $20.

Does this count new spending on the card?

No. This counts the balance you type, with no new spending added. Any new spending adds to the balance and pushes the payoff date later.

Is this financial advice?

No. This is an estimate for planning, from the numbers you type. Card rates and fees change, so check your own card statement before you act.
