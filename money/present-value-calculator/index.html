2Quick answer

The present value of a future sum is the future amount divided by one plus the discount rate, raised to the number of years. For example, $1,000.00 in 10 years at a discount rate of 5.00% a year is worth $613.91 today. Type your own future amount, rate and years above.

3The formula

Money in the future is worth less than money today, because money today can earn interest. The discount rate is how much less it is worth each year:

Present value — in words: divide the future amount by one plus the yearly discount rate, raised to the power of the number of years. present value = future amount ÷ (1 + rate)^years

Formula source: the standard present value (discounting) formula. No live rate is used — the discount rate is the one you type.

4Worked example: $1,000.00 in 10 years at 5.00%

  1. Start with the figures: a future amount of $1,000.00, a discount rate of 5.00% a year, 10 years from now.
  2. Find the discount factor: (1 + 5.00 ÷ 100)^10 = 1.05^10 = 1.62889.
  3. Divide the future amount by the factor: $1,000.00 ÷ 1.62889 = $613.91.
  4. Answer: the present value is $613.91 — the discount is $1,000.00 − $613.91 = $386.09.

Try it in the tool above: type 1000, 5 and 10, and the result line shows $613.91.

6Questions about present value

What does present value mean in plain words?

It is what a future sum of money is worth today. If you will receive $1,000.00 in 10 years, and money can earn 5.00% a year, that future $1,000.00 is worth $613.91 today — because $613.91 invested now at 5.00% would grow to $1,000.00 in 10 years.

What discount rate should I type?

Type the yearly rate that matches your question: the interest you could earn, the inflation rate, or the return you want from an investment. A higher rate gives a lower present value, because the future money is discounted more heavily.

What if the discount rate is zero?

Then the present value equals the future amount exactly, because money is not discounted at all. For example, $5,000.00 in 3 years at 0% is worth $5,000.00 today.

How is present value different from future value?

They are the same idea in opposite directions. Present value works backwards: a future amount is discounted to today. Future value works forwards: a sum today is grown to a future date. The Future Value Calculator on this site does the forward sum.

Why is the present value always smaller than the future amount?

As long as the discount rate is above zero, the discount factor is above one, so dividing by it always gives a smaller number. The longer the wait, or the higher the rate, the smaller the present value.
