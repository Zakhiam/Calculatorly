2Quick answer

On a monthly income of $4,000 after tax, the 50/30/20 budget gives $2,000.00 for needs, $1,200.00 for wants and $800.00 for savings. Needs are the bills you must pay, like rent and food. Wants are the extras you enjoy, like eating out. Savings cover debt payoff and money put aside for later.

3The formula

The 50/30/20 rule splits income after tax into three parts that always add up to the whole income:

50/30/20 budget — in words: half of your income after tax goes to needs, three tenths to wants, and two tenths to savings. needs = income × 50 ÷ 100; wants = income × 30 ÷ 100; savings = income × 20 ÷ 100

Formula source: the 50/30/20 budgeting rule, a common planning guide. No data is fetched from anywhere — you type your own income, and the page uses only that one number.

4Worked example: $4,000 a month after tax

  1. Start with the number: monthly income after tax is $4,000.
  2. Needs are 50%: $4,000 × 50 ÷ 100 = $2,000.00.
  3. Wants are 30%: $4,000 × 30 ÷ 100 = $1,200.00. Savings are 20%: $4,000 × 20 ÷ 100 = $800.00.
  4. Answer: needs $2,000.00, wants $1,200.00 and savings $800.00 — and $2,000 + $1,200 + $800 = $4,000, the whole income.

6Questions about budget 50/30/20

What counts as a need?

A need is a bill you must pay to live and work: rent or mortgage, food, power, water, transport to work, insurance and minimum debt payments. If you stopped paying it, life would break down quickly.

What counts as a want?

A want is spending you enjoy but could live without: eating out, films, holidays, new clothes you do not need, and streaming services. Wants are not bad — they are planned, at 30%, instead of taking money from savings.

What counts as savings?

Savings are money put aside for later: an emergency fund, extra debt payoff above the minimum, and money for retirement or a deposit. Paying off a costly debt counts here, because it saves interest.

What if my needs cost more than 50%?

That is common where rent is high. Cut wants first, then look for a cheaper need — a smaller home, a cheaper phone plan — and raise income if you can. The 50% figure is a target, not a pass mark.

Should the split use income before or after tax?

After tax, because that is the money that actually reaches you. Using income before tax would plan spending with money you never see.

Is this financial advice?

No. This is a planning guide, from the number you type. A budget that fits your real bills matters more than hitting 50/30/20 exactly.
