2Quick answer

The payback period is the investment divided by the cash that comes back each year. For example, a $10,000.00 investment with $4,000.00 coming back each year pays for itself in 2.50 years, which is 2 years and 6 months. Type your own investment and yearly cash above.

3The formula

The payback period is the simplest break-even measure: how long until the money coming back has covered the money that went out. It assumes the same cash comes back every year:

Payback period — in words: divide the investment by the cash that comes back each year, then split the answer into whole years and the months left over. payback years = investment ÷ yearly cash flow; months = (payback years − whole years) × 12

Formula source: the standard simple payback definition (investment divided by yearly cash flow). No rate is taken from anywhere else — only the two amounts you type.

4Worked example: $10,000.00 invested, $4,000.00 a year back

  1. Start with the figures: investment $10,000.00, cash back $4,000.00 each year.
  2. Divide the investment by the yearly cash: $10,000.00 ÷ $4,000.00 = 2.50 years.
  3. Split into years and months: 2 whole years, and 0.50 × 12 = 6 months left over.
  4. Answer: the payback period is 2 years, 6 months.

Try it in the tool above: type 10000 and 4000, and the result line shows 2 years, 6 months.

6Questions about payback period

What does the payback period tell me?

It tells you how long it takes for an investment to pay for itself. A shorter payback means the money comes back sooner, which is less risky. It does not tell you how much profit the investment makes after it has paid for itself — for that, use the ROI Calculator or the IRR Calculator.

Does the payback period allow for the time value of money?

No. This is the simple payback: it treats $1,000.00 back in year five as equal to $1,000.00 back in year one. A discounted payback would allow for that, but it needs a discount rate. If the timing matters to you, check the IRR Calculator as well.

What if the cash coming back changes from year to year?

This calculator assumes the same cash comes back every year. If the cash changes, add the yearly amounts up by hand until the total reaches the investment — the year it reaches it is the payback year. The IRR Calculator handles uneven cash flows properly.

What if the yearly cash is zero or negative?

Then the investment never pays for itself, and the calculator says so plainly instead of giving a made-up number. The yearly cash must be above zero for a payback period to exist.

Is a shorter payback always better?

Not always. A short payback is safer, because the money comes back sooner, but a longer project can still earn more in total once it has paid for itself. Use the payback period together with ROI or IRR, not on its own.

Is this calculator free, and do I need to sign up?

Yes, it is free, and no sign-up is needed. Nothing is uploaded: the calculation runs in your browser on your own device, and the numbers you type are not sent anywhere.
