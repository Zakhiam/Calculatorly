2Quick answer

The total cost of college is the yearly tuition plus the yearly living costs, minus any financial aid per year, multiplied by the number of years. For example, tuition of $10,000.00 and living costs of $8,000.00 a year for 4 years, less $2,000.00 a year of aid, come to $64,000.00 in total. Type your own figures above.

3The formula

College cost is a simple yearly total, repeated for each year of the degree. Aid lowers the yearly total before the years are counted:

Total college cost — in words: add the yearly tuition and the yearly living costs, subtract the yearly financial aid, then multiply by the number of years. total = (tuition + living − aid) × years

Formula source: plain addition and multiplication of the figures you type. No fee, price or rate is taken from anywhere else — every cost is your own figure.

4Worked example: $10,000.00 tuition, $8,000.00 living, 4 years, $2,000.00 aid

  1. Start with the yearly figures: tuition $10,000.00, living costs $8,000.00, aid $2,000.00, over 4 years.
  2. Find the yearly total: $10,000.00 + $8,000.00 − $2,000.00 = $16,000.00 a year.
  3. Multiply by the years: $16,000.00 × 4 = $64,000.00.
  4. Answer: the total cost of college is $64,000.00.

Try it in the tool above: type 10000, 8000, 4 and 2000, and the result line shows $64,000.00.

6Questions about college cost

What counts as a living cost here?

Anything you pay to live while you study: rent, food, transport, books and other day-to-day costs. Add them together into one yearly living-cost figure. The calculator only needs that one number, so you can group the costs however you like.

What is financial aid in this calculator?

Any money that lowers what you pay each year, such as a grant, a scholarship or a bursary. Type it as a yearly amount. If you have no aid, leave it at 0. Aid that is more than the yearly tuition plus living costs shows a clear message, because the total would be below zero.

Does this include interest on a student loan?

No. This page adds up the cost of the degree itself. If you borrow to pay for it, the interest is a separate cost — use the Student Loan Calculator to work out the monthly payment and the total interest on the loan.

Can I use this for a course that is not 4 years?

Yes. Type any number of years from 1 to 10, such as 1 for a short course, 2 for a diploma or 3 for a degree. The total is the yearly total multiplied by the years you type.

What if the costs change from year to year?

This calculator uses one yearly figure for each cost, so it suits costs that stay roughly the same. If the fees rise each year, work out each year separately and add the yearly totals together by hand, or use the average yearly figure here as a rough guide.

Is this calculator free, and do I need to sign up?

Yes, it is free, and no sign-up is needed. Nothing is uploaded: the calculation runs in your browser on your own device, and the numbers you type are not sent anywhere.
