2Quick answer

With simple interest, the interest is always worked out on the starting amount only, so it grows by the same amount every year. For example, 1,000 at 5% a year for 3 years earns 150 of interest, so the total is 1,150. Type your own starting amount, rate and years above and this calculator gives the interest and the total.

3The formula

Simple interest never earns interest on itself, so the sum is a straight multiplication:

Simple interest — in words: multiply the starting amount by the yearly rate as a share, then multiply by the number of years. interest = P × r ÷ 100 × t, and total = P + interest

Formula source: the standard simple interest formula. The interest rate is typed by you — this site does not fetch live savings rates, and no tax is taken off here. This is an estimate only, not professional advice.

4Worked example: 1,000 at 5% for 3 years

6Questions about simple interest

What is the difference between simple and compound interest?

Simple interest is paid only on the starting amount, so it grows by the same amount every year. Compound interest is paid on the starting amount plus all the interest earned so far, so it grows by more each year. Simple interest always gives the smaller total over time.

When is simple interest used?

On short loans and some savings products where the interest is paid out rather than added to the balance. Most bank savings accounts use compound interest instead, because the interest stays in the account and earns more.

Can the time be a part of a year?

Yes. Type 2.5 for two and a half years: the interest is worked out for exactly that time. For example, 2,500 at 8% for 2.5 years earns 500 of interest.

Is tax taken off the interest here?

No. The figures are before any tax on interest, and before any fees or withdrawals. Your bank or tax authority can tell you what tax applies to your interest.

What if the interest rate is zero?

The interest is 0.00 and the total is exactly the starting amount. The calculator shows that as a real answer, not an error.

How are the answers rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. The same rule is used in the answer, the bar and the table, so the figures always agree.
