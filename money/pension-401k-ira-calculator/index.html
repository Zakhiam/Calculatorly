2Quick answer

A pension, 401(k), IRA, super or RRSP grows from two parts: the monthly payments, which build up with compound growth, and any savings you already have, which grow on their own. For example, paying £200.00 a month for 30 years at 5.00% yearly growth gives £166,451.73. Pick your country above and type your own figures.

3The formula

Growth compounds every month, so money paid in early has the longest time to grow. The rate is the one you type — this page never takes a rate from anywhere else:

Projected pension value — in words: each monthly payment grows with compound interest until retirement, the savings you already have grow the same way, and the two parts are added together. value = monthly × (((1 + r)^n − 1) ÷ r) + savings × (1 + r)^n, where r is the monthly growth rate and n is the number of months

Formula source: the standard future value of regular monthly payments plus the future value of a lump sum, with monthly compounding. No official rate is used. This is an estimate only, not professional advice.

4Worked example: UK pension, £200.00 a month, 5.00% growth, 30 years

  1. Start with the figures: a UK pension, £200.00 a month, 5.00% yearly growth, 30 years, no savings yet.
  2. Find the monthly growth rate: 5.00 ÷ 100 ÷ 12 = 0.0041667, and the number of months: 30 × 12 = 360.
  3. Grow the payments: £200.00 × (((1.0041667)^360 − 1) ÷ 0.0041667) = £166,451.73.
  4. Total paid in is £200.00 × 360 = £72,000.00, so the growth is £166,451.73 − £72,000.00 = £94,451.73.
  5. Answer: the projected value is £166,451.73.

Try it in the tool above: pick UK, type 200, 5, 30 and 0, and the result line shows £166,451.73.

6Questions about pension 401(k) ira

What is the difference between a pension, a 401(k), an IRA, super and an RRSP?

They are the same idea in different countries: a long-term retirement savings account. The UK has pensions, the USA has 401(k) plans and IRAs, Australia has superannuation (super) and Canada has RRSPs. The maths on this page is the same for all of them — only the currency symbol and the country note change.

Why does the country choice matter?

Each country has its own rules: yearly contribution limits, tax relief and the age you can take the money out. This calculator does not work out tax or limits — it only projects the value. The short note in the table under the result names the rule to check for the country you pick.

What growth rate should I type?

Type the yearly growth rate you want to plan with, as a percent. The calculator accepts 0% to 30%. Growth is never promised: markets go up and down, and past growth does not guarantee future growth. Try a lower rate as well, so you can see a careful case next to a hopeful one.

Does this include tax, fees or inflation?

No. The result is the value before tax, before any fees, and in the money of the future, not in today's prices. Tax, fees and inflation all lower what the money will buy, so treat the figure as a planning guide, not a promise. This is an estimate only, not professional advice.

What if I already have savings in my pension?

Type them in the Current savings box. They grow with the same yearly rate for the whole period, and are added to the value of your monthly payments. With no savings yet, leave the box at 0.

Is this calculator free, and do I need to sign up?

Yes, it is free, and no sign-up is needed. Nothing is uploaded: the calculation runs in your browser on your own device, and the numbers you type are not sent anywhere.
