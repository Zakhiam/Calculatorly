2Quick answer

An annuity turns a lump sum into a fixed monthly income, using the same formula as a loan payment. For example, a $100,000.00 lump sum at 5.00% a year over 20 years pays $659.96 a month. The monthly rate is the yearly rate divided by 12. Type your own figures above.

3The formula

An annuity is a loan in reverse: instead of borrowing a lump sum and paying it back month by month, you hand over a lump sum and receive the same monthly amount back:

Monthly annuity income — in words: divide the yearly rate by 12 to get the monthly rate, then divide the lump sum times that rate by one minus the monthly rate factor over all the months. income = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^(−n)), where r is the monthly rate and n is the number of months

Formula source: the standard fixed-rate annuity (amortisation) formula. No live rate is used — the interest rate is the one you type.

4Worked example: $100,000.00 at 5.00% over 20 years

  1. Start with the lump sum: $100,000.00, a yearly rate of 5.00%, payments over 20 years.
  2. Find the monthly rate: 5.00 ÷ 100 ÷ 12 = 0.0041667, and the number of months: 20 × 12 = 240.
  3. Apply the formula: $100,000.00 × 0.0041667 ÷ (1 − (1.0041667)^(−240)) = $659.96.
  4. Total income over the term: $659.96 × 240 = $158,389.39, so the interest part is $158,389.39 − $100,000.00 = $58,389.39.
  5. Answer: the monthly income is $659.96.

6Questions about annuity

What is an annuity?

An annuity is an agreement where you pay a lump sum once, and in return you receive a fixed income every month for a set number of years. It is often used at retirement, to turn savings into a steady income.

How is the monthly income worked out?

With the same formula as a fixed-rate loan payment: the yearly rate is divided by 12 to get the monthly rate, and the income is the lump sum times that rate, divided by one minus the discount factor over the number of months.

What happens if the interest rate is zero?

Then the lump sum is simply split evenly over the months. For example, a $120,000.00 lump sum over 10 years with no interest pays $120,000.00 ÷ 120 = $1,000.00 a month, and the total income equals the lump sum.

Does a longer payment term give more total income?

Yes, if the rate stays the same, because interest has more time to build up — but the monthly income is smaller, because the same lump sum is spread over more months. The table under the result shows both the monthly income and the total income, so you can compare terms.

Is this an estimate or a guaranteed income?

It is an estimate from the rate you type. A real annuity rate is set by the provider and depends on your age, health and the terms of the agreement, and the income may be taxed. This is an estimate only, not professional advice.
