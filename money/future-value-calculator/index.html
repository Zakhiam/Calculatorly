2Quick answer

The future value of a sum is the amount today multiplied by one plus the yearly growth rate, raised to the number of years. For example, $1,000.00 at 5.00% a year for 10 years grows to $1,628.89. Type your own amount, rate and years above.

3The formula

Growth compounds: each year the amount grows, and the next year the growth itself earns growth. That is why the power in the formula matters:

Future value — in words: multiply the amount today by one plus the yearly growth rate, raised to the power of the number of years. future value = present amount × (1 + rate)^years

Formula source: the standard future value (compound growth) formula. No live rate is used — the growth rate is the one you type.

4Worked example: $1,000.00 at 5.00% for 10 years

  1. Start with the figures: $1,000.00 today, a growth rate of 5.00% a year, 10 years.
  2. Find the growth factor: (1 + 5.00 ÷ 100)^10 = 1.05^10 = 1.62889.
  3. Multiply the amount by the factor: $1,000.00 × 1.62889 = $1,628.89.
  4. Answer: the future value is $1,628.89 — the growth is $1,628.89 − $1,000.00 = $628.89.

Try it in the tool above: type 1000, 5 and 10, and the result line shows $1,628.89.

6Questions about future value

What does future value mean in plain words?

It is what a sum of money today will be worth after a number of years at a steady growth rate. For example, $1,000.00 at 5.00% a year for 10 years grows to $1,628.89, because the interest earns interest of its own each year.

What growth rate should I type?

Type the yearly rate you want to plan with: a savings rate, an investment return, or the inflation rate if you want to see what a sum must grow to just to keep its value. A higher rate gives a higher future value.

What if the growth rate is zero?

Then the future value equals the amount today exactly, because nothing is added. For example, $2,000.00 at 0% for 10 years stays $2,000.00.

How is future value different from present value?

They are the same idea in opposite directions. Future value works forwards: a sum today is grown to a future date. Present value works backwards: a future amount is discounted to today. The Present Value Calculator on this site does the backward sum.

Does this include tax, fees or inflation?

No. The result is the value before tax and before fees, and it does not allow for inflation unless you use an inflation rate as the growth rate. Treat the figure as a planning guide, not a promise.

Is this calculator free, and do I need to sign up?

Yes, it is free, and no sign-up is needed. Nothing is uploaded: the calculation runs in your browser on your own device, and the numbers you type are not sent anywhere.
