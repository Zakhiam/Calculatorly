2Quick answer

To reach a savings goal, save a fixed amount each month and let the interest help you. For example, to reach 10,000 in 5 years at 4% a year, save about 150.83 a month. Type your own target, years and rate above and this calculator gives the monthly amount, the total you pay in and the interest earned.

3The formula

Each monthly saving earns interest from the month it is paid in, so the interest does part of the work for you. The formula is:

Monthly saving for a goal — in words: take the monthly interest rate, multiply it by the target, and divide by the monthly rate factor raised to the number of months, minus one. monthly saving = target × r ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1), where r is the monthly rate and n is the number of months

Formula source: the standard future value of an annuity formula, with payments at the end of each month. The interest rate is typed by you — this site does not fetch live savings rates. This is an estimate only, not professional advice.

4Worked example: reach 10,000 in 5 years at 4%

  1. Start with the figures: target 10,000, 5 years (60 months), yearly rate 4%.
  2. Work out the monthly rate: 4 ÷ 100 ÷ 12 = 0.003333.
  3. Put the numbers in the formula: 10,000 × 0.003333 ÷ ((1.003333)^60 − 1).
  4. Answer: save 150.83 a month. Over 60 months you pay in 9,049.91 and the interest adds 950.09.

Try it in the tool above: type 10000, 5 and 4, and the result line shows 150.83.

6Questions about savings goal

Does the interest really lower what I must save?

Yes. Without interest you would have to save 10,000 ÷ 60 = 166.67 a month. At 4% a year the interest adds about 950 over 5 years, so you only need to save 150.83 a month.

What if the interest rate is zero?

Then the interest adds nothing, so the monthly saving is simply the target divided by the number of months: 5,000 over 3 years is 138.89 a month.

What happens if I save for longer?

The monthly amount falls twice over: you spread the target over more months, and the interest has more time to work. Try 10 years instead of 5 above to see the difference.

Are the savings paid at the start or the end of the month?

This calculator counts each saving at the end of the month, which is how most regular savings plans work. Paying at the start of the month would earn a little more interest, so this figure is the cautious one.

Is tax taken off the interest here?

No. The figures are before any tax on interest, and before any fees. Your bank or tax authority can tell you what tax applies to your interest.

How are the answers rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. Each line is rounded on its own for display, so multiplying the rounded monthly saving by the number of months can differ by a few cents from the total shown.
