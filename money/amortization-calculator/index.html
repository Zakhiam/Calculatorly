2Quick answer

A loan of $200,000 at 5% a year over 30 years costs $1,073.64 a month. The monthly payment comes from the loan amount, the monthly rate and the number of payments. Each payment first covers that month's interest, and the rest cuts the balance. Over 30 years you pay $386,511.57 in total, of which $186,511.57 is interest.

3The formula

A fixed-rate loan is paid off in equal monthly payments. The payment is set so the last payment clears the balance exactly:

Monthly loan payment — in words: the loan amount times the monthly rate, divided by one minus the monthly rate factor for the number of payments. payment = P × r ÷ (1 − (1 + r)^−n), where r is the monthly rate and n is the number of monthly payments

Formula source: the standard amortisation formula for a fixed-rate loan. No rate is fetched from anywhere — you type your own rate, and the page uses only the three numbers you type.

4Worked example: $200,000 at 5% over 30 years

  1. Start with the loan: $200,000, a yearly rate of 5% and a term of 30 years (360 monthly payments).
  2. Turn the yearly rate into a monthly rate: 5% ÷ 12 = 0.4167% a month, or 0.004167 as a decimal.
  3. Put the numbers in the formula: payment = 200,000 × 0.004167 ÷ (1 − (1 + 0.004167)^−360) = $1,073.64.
  4. Answer: the monthly payment is $1,073.64. In month 1, interest is $833.33 and principal is $240.31, so the balance falls to $199,759.69.

Try it in the tool above: type 200000, 5 and 30, and the result line shows $1,073.64.

6Questions about amortization

How is the monthly payment worked out?

The payment comes from the amortisation formula shown on this page: the loan amount, the monthly interest rate and the number of payments. The same payment is due every month for the whole term, so the loan is cleared by the last payment.

Why is most of the first payment interest?

Interest is charged on the balance you still owe, so it is highest at the start when the balance is largest. As the balance falls, the interest part of each payment falls and the principal part grows. That is why the balance falls slowly at first and faster later.

What happens if the interest rate is zero?

With a 0% rate there is no interest at all, so the payment is simply the loan amount divided by the number of months. For example, $120,000 over 10 years at 0% costs $1,000.00 a month.

Does extra payment change the result?

This calculator shows the plan with no extra payments. Paying extra in real life cuts the balance faster, so less interest builds up and the loan ends sooner. Work out the new term by typing a smaller loan amount or a shorter term.

How is the total interest worked out?

Total interest is the monthly payment times the number of payments, minus the loan amount. For $200,000 at 5% over 30 years that is 360 × $1,073.64 − $200,000 = $186,511.57.

Is this financial advice?

No. This is an estimate for planning, worked out from the numbers you type. A real loan also has fees, insurance and terms that change the cost, so check with the lender before you borrow.
