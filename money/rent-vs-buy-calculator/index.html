2Quick answer

With rent of $1,500 a month and a $300,000 home bought with a $60,000 deposit at 5% over 10 years, renting is cheaper by $34,604.63. Buying costs the deposit plus the monthly mortgage payment for 10 years, while renting costs the rent for 10 years. The break-even year, when the two totals meet, is about 24 years.

3The formula

Both choices are added up over the same number of years. The mortgage payment uses the standard loan formula on a 30-year term:

Rent vs buy — in words: compare the deposit plus the monthly mortgage payment times the months, against the monthly rent times the months. buying cost = deposit + (monthly payment × months); renting cost = rent × months

Formula source: the standard amortisation formula for the mortgage payment, plus simple multiplication for the totals. This is a plain cost comparison: it leaves out house price growth, rent rises, tax, upkeep and selling costs, which all change the real answer.

4Worked example: rent $1,500 or buy a $300,000 home

  1. Start with the numbers: rent $1,500 a month, home price $300,000, deposit $60,000, mortgage rate 5%, 10 years to compare.
  2. The loan is $300,000 − $60,000 = $240,000. On a 30-year term at 5% the monthly payment is $1,288.37.
  3. Buying over 10 years costs $60,000 + 120 × $1,288.37 = $214,604.63. Renting over 10 years costs 120 × $1,500 = $180,000.00.
  4. Answer: renting is cheaper by $34,604.63 over 10 years. The break-even year is about 24 years, when the two totals meet.

6Questions about rent vs buy

What does the break-even year mean?

It is the year when the total cost of buying catches up with the total cost of renting. Before that year renting costs less in total; after it, buying costs less in total. It comes from dividing the deposit by the monthly gap between rent and the mortgage payment.

Why does buying look dearer at first?

Buying starts with a large deposit paid on day one, while renting has no deposit. The mortgage payment also stays the same each month, so buying only wins back the deposit slowly, year by year.

Does this count house prices going up?

No. This is a plain cost comparison with no price growth, no rent rises, no tax, no upkeep and no selling costs. If house prices rise fast or rents rise fast, the real answer can be very different.

What mortgage term is used for the payment?

The monthly payment is worked out on a 30-year term at the rate you type. If your real loan has a different term, the payment changes — a shorter term means a higher payment but less interest over time.

Is renting always cheaper for a short stay?

Usually, yes, because the deposit and the buying costs have little time to be won back. For a stay of only a few years, renting is normally cheaper on this plain comparison.

Is this financial advice?

No. This is an estimate for planning, from the numbers you type. Buying a home is a large decision — check the full costs, including tax and upkeep, with a qualified adviser before you act.
