2Quick answer

Driving 10,000 miles a year costs $1,000.00 in petrol at 35 miles per gallon and $3.50 a gallon, against $450.00 in electricity at 0.30 kWh per mile and $0.15 per kWh. Electric driving costs $550.00 less a year here. All prices are figures you type yourself — this site holds no fuel or electricity prices.

3The formula

Both costs are worked out for the same miles, so the comparison is fair. Only fuel and charging are counted:

Fuel vs EV — in words: yearly petrol cost is miles divided by miles per gallon, times the petrol price; yearly charging cost is miles times kWh per mile, times the electricity price. petrol cost per year = miles ÷ miles per gallon × petrol price; electric cost per year = miles × kWh per mile × electricity price

Formula source: plain multiplication from the figures you type. No fuel price or electricity price is fetched from anywhere — you type your own prices, and the page uses only the five numbers you type. The result leaves out buying, tax, insurance, servicing and charging at home against public chargers.

4Worked example: 10,000 miles a year

  1. Start with the numbers: 10,000 miles a year, petrol car 35 miles per gallon at $3.50 a gallon, electric car 0.30 kWh per mile at $0.15 per kWh.
  2. Petrol: 10,000 ÷ 35 = 285.71 gallons a year, times $3.50 = $1,000.00 a year.
  3. Electric: 10,000 × 0.30 = 3,000 kWh a year, times $0.15 = $450.00 a year.
  4. Answer: petrol $1,000.00 a year, electric $450.00 a year — electric costs $550.00 less.

Try it in the tool above: type 10000, 35, 3.5, 0.3 and 0.15, and the result line shows both yearly costs.

6Questions about fuel vs ev

Where do the fuel and electricity prices come from?

From you. This site holds no fuel or electricity prices and fetches none. Type the prices you actually pay — a home charger is usually much cheaper than a public fast charger.

Why is only fuel counted, not the cost of the car?

Because this tool compares running costs for the same miles. The cost of buying the car, tax, insurance and servicing are left out — an electric car often costs more to buy and less to run, so the full answer needs both sides.

What if I drive few miles?

Then the yearly saving is small, because the saving is per mile. At 3,000 miles a year the saving in the example is only $165.00, which may not cover a dearer car.

What does kWh per mile mean?

It is how much electricity the car uses for each mile — about 0.25 to 0.40 for most electric cars. A bigger or faster car uses more. Your car's own display or handbook gives the figure.

Can I use litres and km instead?

This page uses gallons and miles. To change litres per 100 km into miles per gallon, use the unit converter on this site first, then type the result here.

Is this financial advice?

No. This is an estimate for planning, from the figures you type. Prices change, so check your own fuel and electricity prices before you act.
