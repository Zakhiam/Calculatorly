2Quick answer

On two $20,000 loans over 5 years, Loan A at 6% costs $553.88 more in total interest than Loan B at 5%. Loan A's payment is $386.66 a month with $3,199.36 of interest, while Loan B's payment is $377.42 a month with $2,645.48 of interest. 3The formula

Each loan is worked out on its own with the standard amortisation formula. The comparison is on total interest, not just the monthly payment:

Loan comparison — in words: work out the monthly payment and total interest for each loan with the standard loan formula, then subtract the smaller total interest from the larger. total interest = (monthly payment × number of payments) − loan amount; difference = larger total interest − smaller total interest

Formula source: the standard amortisation formula for a fixed-rate loan. No rate is fetched from anywhere — you type both rates, and the page uses only the six numbers you type.

4Worked example: $20,000 at 6% against $20,000 at 5%

  1. Start with the numbers: both loans are $20,000 over 5 years (60 payments). Loan A is at 6%, Loan B at 5%.
  2. Loan A: the monthly payment is $386.66, so the total paid is 60 × $386.66 = $23,199.36 and the interest is $3,199.36.
  3. Loan B: the monthly payment is $377.42, so the total paid is 60 × $377.42 = $22,645.48 and the interest is $2,645.48.
  4. Answer: Loan A costs $553.88 more in total interest than Loan B.

6Questions about loan comparison

Should I compare the monthly payment or the total interest?

Compare the total interest when the loan amounts are the same — it is the true cost of borrowing. A loan can have a lower monthly payment but cost more in total if it runs for longer.

Can a loan with a higher rate still be cheaper?

Yes, if its term is much shorter. Interest builds up month by month, so a short loan at a higher rate can cost less in total than a long loan at a lower rate. This calculator shows both figures so you can see it.

What if the two loans are for different amounts?

Then compare the total paid for each loan, and think about what you get for the extra money. The verdict here is on total interest, which is fair only when the amounts match — the table shows the monthly payment and total paid for each loan too.

Do fees change the answer?

Yes. Fees are not in this comparison — only the loan amount, rate and term. A loan with a low rate but high fees can cost more in real life, so add the fees to the loan amount before you compare, or count them on their own.

What does total interest leave out?

It leaves out fees, insurance and any late-payment charges. It is the interest alone, worked out from the rate and term you type.

Is this financial advice?

No. This is an estimate for planning, from the numbers you type. Check the full terms with each lender before you borrow.
