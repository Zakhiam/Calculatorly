2Quick answer

Tiles are counted from the floor area and the area of one tile, plus waste for cuts. A 12 square metre floor with 0.3 by 0.3 metre tiles needs 147 tiles with 10% waste allowed. Type your figures above and this calculator shows the tiles and the boxes to buy.

3The formula

Boxes hold 10 tiles, rounded up. The waste allowance covers cuts at edges and corners, and broken tiles.

Tiles needed — in words: divide the floor area by the area of one tile, add the waste allowance, then round up to a whole tile. tiles = floor area ÷ (tile length × tile width) × (1 + waste ÷ 100), rounded up

Formula source: plain arithmetic on the four numbers you type, with 10 tiles per box. No outside data is used. This is an estimate for planning a job.

4Worked example: 12 m² floor, 0.3 m by 0.3 m tiles, 10% waste

  1. Area of one tile: 0.3 × 0.3 = 0.09 m².
  2. Tiles without waste: 12 ÷ 0.09 = 133.33.
  3. With 10% waste: 133.33 × 1.10 = 146.67, rounded up to 147 tiles.
  4. Answer: 147 tiles. Boxes of 10: 147 ÷ 10 = 14.7, rounded up to 15 boxes.

Try it in the tool above: type 12, 0.3, 0.3 and 10, and the result line shows 147 tiles.

6Questions about tile

What is the waste allowance for?

Cuts at edges and corners, tiles broken while cutting, and spares for later repairs. A 10% allowance is normal for a square room. Use 15% for a diagonal pattern or a room with many corners.

How many tiles are in a box?

This page counts 10 tiles per box. Check the box you are buying — some tiles come in boxes of 5, 8 or 12, and the boxes needed change with that.

Can I use centimetres instead of metres?

This page takes metres only. A 30 centimetre tile is 0.3 metres, and a 60 centimetre tile is 0.6 metres. Divide centimetres by 100 to get metres.

What if my floor is not a rectangle?

Work out the area of each part of the floor and add them together, then put the total area in the box. This page works out one area at a time.

Should I buy spare tiles?

Yes. The waste allowance already adds spares, and it is wise to keep a few whole tiles from the same batch for later repairs, because later batches can differ in shade.

Is this calculator free, and do I need to sign up?
