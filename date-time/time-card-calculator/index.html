2Quick answer

A time card adds up the hours from each day's clock-in and clock-out times, less the unpaid break, and multiplies the total by the hourly rate. For example, 09:00 to 17:00 with a 30-minute break is 7.50 hours a day, or 37.50 hours over five days, and at $25.00 an hour the pay is $937.50. Type your own times above.

3The formula

Each day is worked out on its own, then the days are added. Hours above 40 in the week are flagged as overtime, so they stand out in the table:

Time card — in words: for each day, subtract the clock-in time from the clock-out time, take off the unpaid break, add the days together, and multiply the total hours by the hourly rate. hours per day = (clock-out − clock-in) − break; total pay = total hours × hourly rate

Formula source: plain time arithmetic on the clock-in and clock-out times you type, with a 40-hour week as the overtime line. No rate is taken from anywhere else — the hourly rate is the one you type.

4Worked example: 09:00 to 17:00, 30-minute break, $25.00 an hour

  1. Start with one day: clock in at 09:00, clock out at 17:00, with a 30-minute unpaid break.
  2. Find the hours for that day: 17:00 − 09:00 = 8.00 hours, less 0.50 hours of break = 7.50 hours.
  3. Do the same for all five days: 7.50 × 5 = 37.50 hours in total.
  4. Find the pay: 37.50 × $25.00 = $937.50. The week is under 40 hours, so the overtime is 0.00 hours.
  5. Answer: 37.50 hours in total, pay $937.50.

Try it in the tool above: leave the times at 09:00 to 17:00, break 30, rate 25, and the result line shows 37.50 hours.

6Questions about time card

How is overtime worked out?

Any hours above 40 in the week are flagged as overtime. For example, 08:00 to 18:00 with a 60-minute break is 9.00 hours a day, which is 45.00 hours over five days, so 5.00 hours are overtime. The pay shown is the total hours times the rate — if your overtime rate is higher, work out the overtime pay separately.

What if I work different hours on different days?

That is fine: each day has its own clock-in and clock-out boxes, so type the real times for each day. Days you did not work can be left with the same time for clock-in and clock-out — for example, 00:00 for both — which counts as 0.00 hours for that day.

Does the break come off every day?

Yes. The unpaid break you type is taken off each day that has hours. If your break is paid, type 0 for the break, so nothing is taken off. The break must be between 0 and 480 minutes a day.

What if I clock out after midnight?

This calculator covers day shifts where clock-out is later than clock-in on the same day. If your shift runs past midnight, the clock-out time is earlier than the clock-in time, and the calculator shows a clear message naming the day, instead of a made-up number.

Is the pay before or after tax?

Before tax. The pay shown is the gross pay: total hours times the hourly rate, with no tax or other amounts taken off. Your take-home pay will be lower once tax and other amounts are taken off.

Is this calculator free, and do I need to sign up?

Yes, it is free, and no sign-up is needed. Nothing is uploaded: the calculation runs in your browser on your own device, and the numbers you type are not sent anywhere.
