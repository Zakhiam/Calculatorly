2Quick answer

Take-home pay in Australia is your salary less income tax and the 2% Medicare levy. For example, on 80,000 in 2026-27 the income tax is 14,520 and the Medicare levy is 1,600, so the take-home pay is 63,880 a year. Your employer also pays 12% super on top of your salary, which is not taken out of your pay.

3The formula

Income tax and the Medicare levy are both worked out on your salary. The 2026-27 figures are:

Australia take-home pay — in words: work out the income tax on your salary, work out the Medicare levy on your salary, and subtract both from your salary. take-home pay = salary − income tax − Medicare levy

Country: Australia. Tax year 2026-27 (1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027). Source: ATO. Last checked 11 November 2026. This is an estimate only, not professional advice.Resident rates: no tax to $18,200; 15% to $45,000; 30% to $135,000; 37% to $190,000; 45% above. Medicare levy: 2% of taxable income; no levy under $28,011, then 10% of the amount over $28,011 until the full 2% applies at $35,014. Superannuation Guarantee: 12%, paid by your employer on top of your salary. No deductions, offsets, HECS-HELP repayments or Medicare levy surcharge are included.

4Worked example: A$80,000 in 2026-27

  1. Start with the salary: 80,000.
  2. Work out the income tax: no tax on the first 18,200; 15% of (45,000 − 18,200) = 4,020; plus 30% of (80,000 − 45,000) = 10,500, so the tax is 14,520.00.
  3. Work out the Medicare levy: 2% of 80,000 = 1,600.00.
  4. Answer: the take-home pay is 80,000 − 14,520.00 − 1,600.00 = A$63,880.00 a year, or A$5,323.33 a month. Your employer also pays A$9,600.00 of super on top.

Try it in the tool above: type 80000, and the result line shows A$63,880.00.

6Questions about australia take-home pay

What is the Medicare levy?

It is a 2% levy on taxable income that helps pay for Medicare, Australia's public health system. Low-income earners pay less: nothing under $28,011 in 2026-27, then a reduced amount until the full 2% applies at $35,014.

Is super taken out of my take-home pay?

No. The Superannuation Guarantee of 12% is paid by your employer into your super fund on top of your salary. On an 80,000 salary that is 9,600 a year of super, and your take-home pay is worked out before it.

Are HECS-HELP repayments included?

No. Study loan repayments are taken from your pay on top of tax, at rates that depend on your income, so they are not included here. Take them off the take-home pay shown here yourself if they apply to you.

What about the low income tax offset?

It is not included. The offset reduces tax for lower incomes, so this page can overstate the tax a little for salaries under about $66,667. The page says so plainly: no offsets are claimed here.

Does this include salary packaging or other deductions?

No. The tax is worked out on the full salary you type, with no deductions claimed. If you salary package part of your pay, your taxable income is lower and your real take-home pay is higher.

How are the answers rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. Each line — income tax and Medicare levy — is rounded to cents first, and the take-home pay is the salary less those rounded lines, so the figures always agree.
