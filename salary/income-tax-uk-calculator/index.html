2Quick answer

Income tax in the UK is worked out on your salary less your personal allowance, at 20%, 40% and 45% across the bands. For example, on 35,000 in 2026/27 with tax code 1257L, the taxable income is 22,430 after the 12,570 allowance, and the tax is 4,486. Type your salary and tax code number above for your own figure.

4Worked example: £35,000 in 2026/27, tax code 1257L

  1. Start with the salary and code: 35,000, tax code 1257, so the personal allowance is 1257 × 10 = 12,570.
  2. Work out the taxable income: 35,000 − 12,570 = 22,430.
  3. Work out the tax: all 22,430 is in the basic rate, so the tax is 20% of 22,430 = 4,486.
  4. Answer: the income tax is £4,486.00 a year, or £373.83 a month.

6Questions about uk income tax

What is the personal allowance?

It is the amount you can earn before any income tax is due: £12,570 in 2026/27. It comes from your tax code number times £10, so code 1257L gives 1257 × £10 = £12,570.

What happens to the allowance over £100,000?

It falls by £1 for every £2 you earn over £100,000, and it is gone entirely by £125,140. On a salary of £120,000 with code 1257L, the allowance is only £2,570.

What does marginal mean?

Each tax rate applies only to the slice of taxable income inside its band. Moving into the 40% band does not mean you pay 40% on all your income — only the slice above £50,270 is taxed at 40%.

Does this cover Scotland?

No. Scotland sets its own income tax bands for non-savings income, so this page uses the England, Wales and Northern Ireland bands. The personal allowance is the same UK-wide.

Does this include National Insurance?

No. National Insurance is a separate contribution taken from your pay. To see your take-home pay after both income tax and National Insurance, use the UK take-home pay calculator.

How are the answers rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. The tax is rounded to cents, and the monthly figure is the yearly tax divided by 12, rounded the same way, so the figures always agree.
