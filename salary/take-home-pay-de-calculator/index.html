2Quick answer

Take-home pay in Germany is your salary less income tax, the solidarity surcharge and social insurance. For example, on €60,000 in 2026 the income tax is €9,321.04 and social insurance is €12,690.00, so the take-home pay is €37,988.96 a year. The figures are for a single person in tax class I.

3The formula

Income tax uses the official tariff, and social insurance uses fixed rates up to its ceilings. The 2026 figures are:

Germany take-home pay — in words: work out the income tax, work out the solidarity surcharge, work out the social insurance, and subtract all three from your salary. take-home pay = salary − income tax − solidarity surcharge − social insurance

Country: Germany. Tax year 2026 (calendar year). Source: Income Tax Act §32a EStG and the Federal Ministry of Finance (BMF). Last checked 11 November 2026. This is an estimate only, not professional advice. Income tax: the first €12,348 is tax-free (Grundfreibetrag); then the rate rises smoothly from 14% to 42%, 42% to €277,825, 45% above. The €1,230 work-expenses allowance is taken off before the tariff. Solidarity surcharge: 5.5% of income tax, only when the tax is over €20,350, rising gradually. Social insurance (employee): pension 9.3% to €101,400; unemployment 1.3% to €101,400; health 8.75% to €69,750; long-term care 1.8% to €69,750. Single person, tax class I, no church tax. Long-term care is 2.4% for people aged 23 or over without children.

4Worked example: €60,000 in 2026

  1. Start with the salary: 60,000.
  2. Work out the taxable income: 60,000 − 12,348 (basic allowance) − 1,230 (work-expenses allowance) = 46,422.
  3. Work out the income tax on 46,422 with the §32a tariff: the rate rises from 14% to about 33.9% across this income, and the tax comes to 9,321.04. The solidarity surcharge is 0.00, because the tax is below €20,350.
  4. Work out the social insurance: pension 9.3% of 60,000 = 5,580.00; unemployment 1.3% of 60,000 = 780.00; health 8.75% of 60,000 = 5,250.00; long-term care 1.8% of 60,000 = 1,080.00, so the total is 12,690.00.
  5. Answer: the take-home pay is 60,000 − 9,321.04 − 0.00 − 12,690.00 = €37,988.96 a year, or €3,165.75 a month.

6Questions about germany take-home pay

What is a tax class?

Your tax class (Steuerklasse) tells your employer how much tax to take from your pay. Class I is for single people, which is what this page uses. Married couples use classes III, IV or V, and single parents use class II, so their figures are different.

What is the Grundfreibetrag?

It is the basic tax-free allowance: the first €12,348 of your taxable income in 2026 is taxed at 0%. Everyone gets it, and it is taken off before the tariff is applied.

What is social insurance?

It is the mandatory cover taken from your pay: pension, unemployment, health and long-term care insurance. The employee pays about 21% of salary in total, but only up to the ceilings: €101,400 for pension and unemployment, €69,750 for health and care.

What is the solidarity surcharge?

It is a surcharge of 5.5% on your income tax, but most people no longer pay it. In 2026 it only applies when your income tax is over €20,350 a year, and it rises gradually from there.

Does this include church tax?

No. Church tax is an extra charge for members of a registered church, usually 8% or 9% of your income tax depending on the state. It is not included here, so members pay a little more than the figure shown.

How are the answers rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. Each line is rounded to cents first, and the take-home pay is the salary less those rounded lines, so the figures always agree.
