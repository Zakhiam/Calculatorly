2Quick answer

Salary sacrifice cuts your taxable pay by the amount sacrificed, so you pay less income tax and National Insurance. On a salary of £60,000 with £5,000 sacrificed, the saving is £2,100.00. Type your salary and sacrifice above and this calculator shows the saving instantly, with the new take-home pay below.

3The formula

Income tax uses the HMRC 2026/27 bands for England, Wales and Northern Ireland, and National Insurance uses the Class 1 employee rates. The sacrificed amount goes into your pension in full.

Tax and National Insurance saved — in words: work out the tax and National Insurance on the full salary, work out both on the salary after the sacrifice, and take the second away from the first. saving = (tax + National Insurance on full salary) − (tax + National Insurance after sacrifice)

Formula source: HMRC 2026/27 — Personal Allowance £12,570, basic rate 20% to £50,270, higher rate 40% to £125,140, additional rate 45% above; Class 1 National Insurance 8% from £12,570 to £50,270 and 2% above. Last checked 11 November 2026. This is an estimate only, not professional advice.

4Worked example: £60,000 salary, £5,000 sacrificed

  1. Tax and National Insurance on £60,000: income tax £11,432.00 + National Insurance £3,210.60 = £14,642.60.
  2. Tax and National Insurance on £55,000 after the sacrifice: income tax £9,432.00 + National Insurance £3,110.60 = £12,542.60.
  3. Saving: £14,642.60 − £12,542.60 = £2,100.00.
  4. Answer: £2,100.00 saved. Take-home pay falls from £45,357.40 to £42,457.40, and £5,000.00 goes into your pension.

6Questions about uk salary sacrifice

What is salary sacrifice in the UK?

It is an agreement with your employer to give up part of your salary in return for a benefit, most often extra pension contributions. The sacrificed amount is not counted as taxable pay or as pay for National Insurance, so you save both income tax and National Insurance on it.

Why do I save National Insurance as well as tax?

Because National Insurance is charged on the pay after the sacrifice. On a £60,000 salary the saving is £2,000.00 of income tax and £100.00 of National Insurance, which is £2,100.00 in total.

Does my employer save anything too?

Yes. Your employer also pays less employer National Insurance, at 15% on pay above £5,000 a year. Some employers add some of that saving to your pension, but this page counts only your own saving.

Does salary sacrifice affect my state pension or benefits?

It can. A lower salary can reduce pay-linked benefits such as statutory maternity pay or mortgage borrowing, and it can affect the National Insurance record if pay falls below the threshold. Keep your pay above £12,570 a year so the record is protected.

What if my salary is over £100,000?

Then the Personal Allowance tapers away by £1 for every £2 over £100,000, and salary sacrifice can restore some of it. This calculator applies the taper, so the saving shown already includes that effect.

Is this an exact figure for my payslip?

No. This is an estimate only, not professional advice. It uses the 2026/27 HMRC rates for England, Wales and Northern Ireland, and it leaves out student loan repayments, pension tax relief limits and Scottish rates. Check HMRC before acting on a figure.
