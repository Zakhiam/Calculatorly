2Quick answer

Paid time off builds up evenly through the year. With a 25-day allowance and 130 of 260 working days done, you have earned 12.50 days so far. Type your allowance and days worked above and this calculator shows the days you have earned, with the rest of the year below.

3The formula

This is straight-line accrual: each working day earns the same share of the yearly allowance. Some employers count calendar days or round to half days, so check your own policy.

Paid time off earned — in words: divide the working days done so far by the working days in the full year, then multiply by the yearly allowance. days earned = yearly allowance × (working days done ÷ working days in the year)

Formula source: even accrual across the working days in the year, from the numbers you type. No outside data is used. Public holidays are not taken off here — they sit inside the working days you count.

4Worked example: 25 days a year, 130 of 260 days done

  1. Share of the year done: 130 ÷ 260 = 0.50, or half the year.
  2. Days earned: 25 × 0.50 = 12.50 days.
  3. Days still to earn: 25 − 12.50 = 12.50 days.
  4. Answer: 12.50 days earned so far.

Try it in the tool above: type 25, 130 and 260, and the result line shows 12.50 days.

6Questions about pto

Does this count public holidays?

No. Public holidays sit inside the working days you count, so they are not taken off separately. If your employer counts only days you actually work, use that count for the working days in the year.

What if I started part-way through the year?

Then count only the working days from your start date to the end of the year, and use that as the working days in the full year. The days you earn are worked out on the same share of the year.

Can I take holiday I have not earned yet?

That depends on your employer. Some allow taking holiday in advance, some do not, and some take it back from your final pay if you leave early. This calculator only shows what you have earned so far.

What if my allowance is in hours, not days?

Use hours everywhere instead of days: yearly allowance in hours, hours worked so far, and hours in the full year. The same sum gives the hours earned so far.

Does unused holiday carry over to next year?

That depends on your contract and your country. Some employers allow a few days to carry over, some do not. This calculator does not carry anything over — it only works out this year.

Is this calculator free, and do I need to sign up?

Yes, it is free, and no sign-up is needed. Nothing is uploaded: the calculation runs in your browser on your own device, and the numbers you type are not sent anywhere.
