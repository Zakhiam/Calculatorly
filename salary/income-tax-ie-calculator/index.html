2Quick answer

Income tax in Ireland is 20% on income up to the standard rate cut-off point and 40% above it. For example, on €50,000 in 2026 a single person pays €7,200.00: €11,200.00 of tax less €4,000.00 of tax credits. The Universal Social Charge and PRSI are charged separately and are not part of this figure.

4Worked example: €50,000 in 2026

  1. Start with the salary: 50,000.
  2. Work out the tax before credits: 20% of 44,000 = 8,800.00; 40% of (50,000 − 44,000) = 2,400.00, so the total is 11,200.00.
  3. Take off the tax credits: personal €2,000.00 plus employee €2,000.00 = €4,000.00.
  4. Answer: the income tax is 11,200.00 − 4,000.00 = €7,200.00 a year, or €600.00 a month.

6Questions about ireland income tax

What is the standard rate cut-off point?

It is the amount of income taxed at 20% before the 40% rate starts. For a single person in 2026 it is €44,000. A married couple with one income has €53,000, and a one-parent family has €48,000.

What are the tax credits?

Every employee gets a personal tax credit of €2,000 and an employee tax credit of €2,000 in 2026, €4,000 in total. They are taken off your tax bill after the 20% and 40% rates are applied.

Is USC part of my income tax?

No. The Universal Social Charge is a separate charge on your gross income, from 0.5% to 8% depending on how much you earn. PRSI is separate too. The take-home pay page shows all three; this page shows the income tax only.

What if I earn less than the tax credits?

Then you pay no income tax at all. The credits are taken off the tax bill, and the bill cannot go below zero, so a single person pays no income tax on a salary of about €20,000 or less.

Do the rates change every year?

They can. The rates, bands and credits are set in each year's Budget, so check the current figures on revenue.ie. The figures on this page are for 2026 and were last checked on 11 November 2026.

How are the answers rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. Each band of tax is rounded to cents first, and the total tax is the sum of those rounded bands, so the figures always agree.
