2Quick answer

Income tax in Australia is worked out on your salary at resident rates from 0% to 45%, with the first 18,200 tax-free. For example, on 80,000 in 2026-27 the tax is 14,520. Type your salary above for your own figure, worked out with no deductions or offsets claimed, and with the Medicare levy not included.

4Worked example: A$80,000 in 2026-27

  1. Start with the salary: 80,000.
  2. Work out the tax on the first slice: no tax on the first 18,200.
  3. Work out the tax on the next slices: 15% of (45,000 − 18,200) = 4,020, plus 30% of (80,000 − 45,000) = 10,500.
  4. Answer: the income tax is 4,020 + 10,500 = A$14,520.00 a year, or A$1,210.00 a month.

6Questions about australia income tax

What is the tax-free threshold?

It is the first $18,200 of income for Australian residents, on which no income tax is paid. Income above that is taxed at the marginal rates, starting at 15% in 2026-27.

What changed in 2026-27?

The rate on income from $18,201 to $45,000 fell from 16% to 15% on 1 July 2026. The other rates and thresholds did not change. This page uses the 2026-27 rates.

What does marginal mean?

Each tax rate applies only to the slice of income inside its bracket. Moving into the 30% bracket does not mean you pay 30% on all your income — only the slice above $45,000 is taxed at 30%.

Is the Medicare levy included?

No. The Medicare levy of 2% is a separate charge on taxable income. To see your take-home pay after both income tax and the levy, use the Australia take-home pay calculator.

Are deductions or offsets claimed here?

No. The tax is worked out on the full salary you type, with no deductions, no low income tax offset and no other offset claimed. If you claim deductions, your real tax is lower.

How are the answers rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. The tax is rounded to cents, and the monthly figure is the yearly tax divided by 12, rounded the same way, so the figures always agree.
