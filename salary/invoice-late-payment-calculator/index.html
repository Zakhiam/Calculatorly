2Quick answer

Late payment interest grows with the amount, the rate and the days late. A $5,000 invoice at 8% a year, paid 30 days late, owes $32.88 in interest. Type your invoice, rate and dates above and this calculator shows the interest owed instantly.

3The formula

The days late are counted from the day after the due date to the payment date. Interest is simple, not compound: it is charged on the invoice amount only, not on earlier interest.

Late payment interest — in words: multiply the invoice amount by the yearly rate, then by the days late divided by 365. interest = amount × (rate ÷ 100) × (days late ÷ 365)

Formula source: simple interest on the numbers you type, with a 365-day year. The rate is the one you type — in the UK the statutory rate is 8% over the Bank of England base rate, but your contract can set another rate. Last checked 11 November 2026.

4Worked example: $5,000 at 8%, 30 days late

  1. Days late: 1 October 2026 minus 1 September 2026 = 30 days.
  2. Yearly interest: $5,000 × 8% = $400.00 a year.
  3. Interest for 30 days: $400.00 × 30 ÷ 365 = $32.88.
  4. Answer: $32.88.

Try it in the tool above: type 5000 and 8, pick 1 September 2026 and 1 October 2026, and the result line shows $32.88.

6Questions about invoice late payment

What interest rate should I use?

Use the rate in your contract or your terms. In the UK the statutory late payment rate is 8% over the Bank of England base rate. In Australia and the USA there is no single statutory rate, so the contract rate applies.

How are the days late counted?

From the day after the due date up to the payment date. A payment made on the due date is not late, and the interest is $0.00. A payment made before the due date is not late either.

Is the interest compound or simple?

Simple. This calculator charges interest on the invoice amount only, for the days late. It does not add interest on top of interest.

Can I charge a fixed late fee as well?

That depends on your contract and your country. In the UK, late payment law also allows fixed compensation of £40 to £100 per invoice. This page works out interest only — add any fixed fee by hand.

What if only part of the invoice was paid late?

Work out the interest on the part that was late, using that part as the amount. If different parts were paid on different dates, work each part out on its own and add them together.

Is this calculator free, and do I need to sign up?

Yes, it is free, and no sign-up is needed. Nothing is uploaded: the calculation runs in your browser on your own device, and the numbers you type are not sent anywhere.
