2Quick answer

Take-home pay in the UK is your salary less income tax and employee National Insurance. For example, on 35,000 in 2026/27 with tax code 1257L, the income tax is 4,486 and the National Insurance is 1,794.40, so the take-home pay is 28,719.60 a year. Type your salary and tax code number above for your own figure.

3The formula

Income tax is worked out on your salary less the personal allowance, and National Insurance is worked out on your salary. The 2026/27 figures are:

UK take-home pay — in words: subtract the personal allowance from your salary, work out the income tax on what is left, work out National Insurance on your salary, and subtract both from your salary. take-home pay = salary − income tax − National Insurance

Country: United Kingdom. Tax year 2026/27 (6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027). Source: HMRC. Last checked 11 November 2026. The allowance falls by £1 for every £2 over £100,000. National Insurance (employee, Class 1): 8% on earnings from £12,570 to £50,270 a year, 2% above. Scotland uses its own income tax bands, so this page covers England, Wales and Northern Ireland only.

4Worked example: £35,000 in 2026/27, tax code 1257L

  1. Start with the salary and code: 35,000, tax code 1257, so the personal allowance is 1257 × 10 = 12,570.
  2. Work out the income tax: the taxable income is 35,000 − 12,570 = 22,430, all in the basic rate, so the tax is 20% of 22,430 = 4,486.00.
  3. Work out National Insurance: 8% of (35,000 − 12,570) = 8% of 22,430 = 1,794.40.
  4. Answer: the take-home pay is 35,000 − 4,486.00 − 1,794.40 = £28,719.60 a year, or £2,393.30 a month.

Try it in the tool above: type 35000 and 1257, and the result line shows £28,719.60.

6Questions about uk take-home pay

What is a tax code number?

It is the number in your tax code: 1257 in 1257L means a personal allowance of 1257 × £10 = £12,570. The letter at the end, usually L, is left off here — type the number only. Your code is on your payslip.

What if my tax code is not 1257L?

Type your own code number. A code of 1000 gives a personal allowance of £10,000, and a code of 1500 gives £15,000. If your code ends in M, N, T or K, this simple version does not cover it — check your payslip or HMRC guidance.

Does this cover Scotland?

No. Scotland sets its own income tax bands for non-savings income, so this page uses the England, Wales and Northern Ireland bands. The personal allowance and National Insurance are the same UK-wide.

What is National Insurance?

It is the contribution taken from your pay that counts towards the State Pension and some benefits. Employees pay Class 1 at 8% on earnings from £12,570 to £50,270 a year, and 2% above that, in 2026/27. Your employer pays a separate amount on top.

Does this include pension contributions taken from my pay?

No. Pension contributions, student loan repayments and any other payroll deductions are not included, because they differ from person to person. Take them off the take-home pay shown here yourself.

How are the answers rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. Each line — income tax and National Insurance — is rounded to cents first, and the take-home pay is the salary less those rounded lines, so the figures always agree.
