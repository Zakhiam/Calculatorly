2Quick answer

Income tax in Singapore is worked out on your chargeable income, which is your salary less your CPF contribution. For example, on a S$60,000 salary in 2026 the chargeable income is S$48,000 and the income tax is S$1,110.00. The first S$20,000 of chargeable income is taxed at 0%, and the top rate of 24% applies only above S$1,000,000.

4Worked example: S$60,000 in 2026

  1. Start with the salary: 60,000.
  2. Take off the CPF contribution: 20% of 60,000 = 12,000.00, so the chargeable income is 60,000 − 12,000.00 = 48,000.
  3. Work out the tax on 48,000: 2% of (30,000 − 20,000) = 200.00; 3.5% of (40,000 − 30,000) = 350.00; 7% of (48,000 − 40,000) = 560.00.
  4. Answer: the income tax is 200.00 + 350.00 + 560.00 = S$1,110.00 a year, or S$92.50 a month.

6Questions about singapore income tax

What is chargeable income?

It is the income your tax is worked out on: your salary less the reliefs you claim. On this page the only relief is your CPF contribution, so chargeable income is your salary less 20% CPF, up to the $96,000 ceiling.

What are the tax rates?

Residents pay 0% on the first $20,000 of chargeable income, then 2%, 3.5%, 7%, 11.5%, 15%, 18%, 19%, 19.5%, 20%, 22% and 23% in the higher bands, and 24% only on income above $1,000,000.

Do I pay tax every month?

No. Singapore has no monthly PAYE withholding for most employees. You file once a year with IRAS and pay the tax as a lump sum or in 12 monthly instalments starting in May.

Are tax reliefs included?

Only the CPF relief is included. Other reliefs, such as the earned income relief of $1,000, are not included, and all reliefs together are capped at $80,000 a year, so your real tax can be a little lower than the figure shown.

What about non-residents?

Non-residents are taxed differently: usually a flat 15% on employment income or the resident rates, whichever is higher. This page is for residents only.

How are the answers rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. Each band of tax is rounded to cents first, and the total tax is the sum of those rounded bands, so the figures always agree.
