2Quick answer

Income tax in Germany is worked out on your taxable income with a tariff that rises smoothly from 14% to 42%. For example, on a €60,000 salary in 2026 the taxable income is €46,422 and the income tax is €9,321.04. Social insurance and the solidarity surcharge are charged separately and are not part of this figure.

4Worked example: €60,000 in 2026

  1. Start with the salary: 60,000.
  2. Work out the taxable income: 60,000 − 12,348 (basic allowance) − 1,230 (work-expenses allowance) = 46,422.
  3. Work out the tax on 46,422 with the §32a tariff: the rate rises from 14% to about 33.9% across this income, and the tax comes to 9,321.04.
  4. Answer: the income tax is €9,321.04 a year, or €776.75 a month.

6Questions about germany income tax

Why is there no simple tax table?

Germany does not use flat bands like most countries. The rate rises smoothly along a curve set by law (§32a EStG), from 14% just above the tax-free allowance to 42% at €69,879, then stays flat at 42% to €277,825 and 45% above that.

What is the Grundfreibetrag?

It is the basic tax-free allowance: the first €12,348 of your taxable income in 2026 is taxed at 0%. The €1,230 work-expenses allowance is also taken off before the tariff is applied.

Is social insurance part of my income tax?

No. Social insurance (pension, unemployment, health and long-term care) is about 21% of your salary on top of the tax, up to its ceilings. The take-home pay page shows both; this page shows the income tax only.

What is the solidarity surcharge?

It is a surcharge of 5.5% on your income tax, but most people no longer pay it. In 2026 it only applies when your income tax is over €20,350 a year, and it rises gradually from there.

What about married couples?

Married couples can be taxed together, which usually lowers the bill. This page is for a single person in tax class I, so if you are married, your real tax is different from the figure shown.

How are the answers rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. Each band of tax is rounded to cents first, and the total tax is the sum of those rounded bands, so the figures always agree.
