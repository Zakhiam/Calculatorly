2Quick answer

The same salary number leaves different take-home pay in each country, because the tax bands and payroll charges differ. On 60,000, the USA leaves $50,390.00, Australia leaves A$50,280.00, Canada leaves C$49,467.52 and the UK leaves £45,357.40. Type a salary above to compare all four countries at once.

3The formula

The same number is used in each country's currency: 60,000 means £60,000 in the UK, $60,000 in the USA, A$60,000 in Australia and C$60,000 in Canada. No currency change is applied, so this is a tax comparison, not a cost-of-living comparison.

Take-home pay — in words: take the gross salary, take off income tax, then take off the payroll charges for that country. take-home pay = gross salary − income tax − payroll charges

Formula source: UK 2026/27 HMRC (income tax bands and Class 1 National Insurance 8% to £50,270, 2% above); Australia 2026–27 ATO (resident rates plus 2% Medicare levy); USA 2026 IRS (single, standard deduction $16,100, Social Security 6.2% to $184,500, Medicare 1.45% plus 0.9% over $200,000); Canada 2026 CRA (federal tax only, CPP 5.95% to $74,600 and CPP2 4% to $85,000, EI 1.63% to $68,900). Last checked 11 November 2026. This is an estimate only, not professional advice.

6Questions about salary country comparison

Why is the same number used in each currency?

So the tax systems are compared on equal terms. A currency change would mix the exchange rate into the answer and hide the tax difference. This page compares what each tax system takes from the same salary number.

Does this include state or provincial tax?

No. Only the national tax and payroll charges are counted: no US state tax, no Canadian provincial tax, no Scottish rates. Those would change the answer, so add them by hand for the place you mean.

Why does Canada show federal tax only?

Because provincial tax varies by province. The federal part is the same across Canada, so it is the fair comparison. A province such as Ontario would take more on top.

What is left out of these figures?

Student loan repayments, pension contributions, the Medicare levy surcharge, the low income tax offset, and tax credits beyond the basic amounts named on this page. The figures are estimates for planning, not a payslip.

Can I compare a salary in one currency only?

Yes, but only as a rough guide. Convert the salary to each currency first, then type that number. The page itself does no currency change.

Is this professional tax advice?

No. This is an estimate only, not professional advice. It uses the tax years and sources named on this page, last checked 11 November 2026. Check HMRC, the IRS, the ATO or the CRA before acting on a figure.
