2Quick answer

A freelancer must cover both the income they want and their business costs, from the hours they can actually bill. A $60,000 income plus $12,000 of costs over 1,200 billable hours needs a rate of $60.00 an hour. Type your own figures above and this calculator shows the rate you need.

3The formula

Billable hours are only the hours a client pays for. Time spent finding work, doing admin and learning is not billable, so it does not sit in this sum.

Freelance hourly rate — in words: add the target yearly income and the yearly business costs, then divide by the billable hours in the year. hourly rate = (target income + yearly costs) ÷ billable hours

Formula source: plain arithmetic on the three numbers you type. No rate, tax table or outside data is used. Tax, pension and holiday pay are not taken off here — add them to your target income if you want them covered.

4Worked example: $60,000 income, $12,000 costs, 1,200 hours

  1. Yearly total to cover: $60,000 + $12,000 = $72,000.
  2. Billable hours in the year: 1,200.
  3. Hourly rate: $72,000 ÷ 1,200 = $60.00.
  4. Answer: $60.00 per hour.

Try it in the tool above: type 60000, 12000 and 1200, and the result line shows $60.00 per hour.

6Questions about freelance rate

What counts as a business cost?

Anything the work needs that an employer would normally pay: software, equipment, insurance, travel, phone, accounting and training. Add them all up for the year and put the total in the costs box.

How many billable hours should I count?

Only the hours a client pays for. A common rule is about 1,200 to 1,400 billable hours in a full year, once holidays, admin and finding work are taken out. Count your own hours honestly — too many hours gives a rate that is too low.

Should I add tax on top of this rate?

Yes, in your own planning. This page works out the rate before tax. If you want $60,000 after tax, set the target income to the before-tax figure that leaves $60,000 after tax.

What if I only work part-time?

Use your part-time figures: the income you want from that work, the costs for that work, and the billable hours you will actually do. The same sum gives the rate for part-time work.

Should I charge this rate to every client?

It is the lowest rate that covers your costs. You can charge more for urgent work, rare skills or short jobs, but charging less than this rate means the work does not pay for itself.

Is this calculator free, and do I need to sign up?

Yes, it is free, and no sign-up is needed. Nothing is uploaded: the calculation runs in your browser on your own device, and the numbers you type are not sent anywhere.
