3The formula

A salary alone can mislead: a lower salary with a bigger bonus and better benefits can be worth more. This page adds the three parts for each offer and compares the totals.

Total value of a job offer — in words: add the yearly salary, the yearly bonus and the yearly value of the benefits. total value = salary + bonus + benefits

Formula source: plain addition of the three yearly amounts you type. No rate, tax table or outside data is used — only the six numbers you type. Tax is not taken off, so compare the totals before tax.

4Worked example: Offer A $68,000 against Offer B $71,000

  1. Offer A: salary $60,000 + bonus $5,000 + benefits $3,000 = $68,000 a year.
  2. Offer B: salary $58,000 + bonus $8,000 + benefits $5,000 = $71,000 a year.
  3. Difference: $71,000 − $68,000 = $3,000.
  4. Answer: Offer B is better by $3,000.00 a year, even though its salary is lower.

Try it in the tool above: type 60000, 5000, 3000 for Offer A and 58000, 8000, 5000 for Offer B, and the result line shows Offer B is better by $3,000.00.

6Questions about job offer comparison

Should I compare salary alone, or the whole offer?

Compare the whole offer. A job with a lower salary can still be worth more once the bonus, pension, health cover and paid time off are added in. This calculator adds salary, bonus and benefits for each offer so the totals can be compared fairly.

How do I put a value on benefits?

Use the yearly cash value: what the benefit would cost you to buy yourself, or what the employer states it is worth. For a pension, use the yearly employer contribution. If a benefit has no clear cash value, leave it out of both offers and weigh it separately.

Does this calculator take tax off the offers?

No. It compares the totals before tax, because tax depends on your full income, your country and your tax code. If the two offers are in different countries, compare them with the Salary Country Comparison Calculator as well.

What if the offers have different working hours?

Then divide each total by its yearly working hours to get the pay per hour. An offer worth $70,000 for a 35-hour week pays more per hour than $72,000 for a 45-hour week. Hours are not part of this page, so work that step out by hand.

What if one offer has a signing bonus?

Add a one-off signing bonus to that offer only in the first year, and say so when you compare. This page treats every amount as yearly, so a one-off bonus added here would overstate the offer in later years.

Is this calculator free, and do I need to sign up?

Yes, it is free, and no sign-up is needed. Nothing is uploaded: the calculation runs in your browser on your own device, and the numbers you type are not sent anywhere.
