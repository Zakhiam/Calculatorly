2Quick answer

To find what percent one number is of another, divide the part by the whole and multiply by 100. For example, 45 is what percent of 180? 45 ÷ 180 × 100 = 25%. Type the part and the whole above and the answer appears instantly, with the bar and table below showing how the share is made up.

3The formula

A percent is a share out of 100, so finding what percent one number is of another means measuring the part against the whole, then scaling that share to hundredths:

What percent one number is of another — in words: divide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100. percent = (part ÷ whole) × 100

Formula source: the standard arithmetic definition of percent (per hundred). No rate, price or outside data is used by this tool — only the two numbers you type.

4Worked example: 45 is what % of 180?

  1. Name the part and the whole: part 45, whole 180.
  2. Divide the part by the whole: 45 ÷ 180 = 0.25.
  3. Multiply by 100 to turn the share into a percent: 0.25 × 100 = 25.
  4. Answer: 45 is 25.00% of 180 — and the rest of the whole is 180 − 45 = 135.

Try it in the tool above: type 45 and 180, and the result line shows 25.00%.

6Questions about what percent

How do I work out what percent one number is of another by hand?

Divide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100. For example, 45 out of 180: 45 ÷ 180 = 0.25, and 0.25 × 100 = 25, so 45 is 25% of 180. This calculator does the same sum instantly as you type.

Can the answer be more than 100%?

Yes, whenever the part is bigger than the whole you compare it with. For example, 150 is 150% of 100, and 90 is 180% of 50. An answer over 100% is a real answer, not an error.

Why does the calculator stop when the whole number is zero?

Because a share of zero cannot be worked out: the sum divides by the whole number, and dividing by zero has no answer in ordinary arithmetic. The calculator shows a clear message instead of a made-up number, and your other entry stays in place so you can fix the zero.

What if the part is zero?

0 is 0.00% of any whole number, because zero is no share at all. That is a real answer, so the calculator shows 0.00% — it only shows an error when the whole number is zero, a box is empty, or a number is above the limit.

How is the answer rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: half-up to 2 decimal places. For example, 1 is 33.333…% of 3, which rounds to 33.33%. The same rule is used in the answer, the bar and the breakdown table, so the figures always agree.
