2Quick answer

The sample size for a survey comes from the confidence level and the margin of error, using the most careful case where half the people answer each way, then adjusted for the population size. For example, a population of 1,000 with 95% confidence and a 5% margin needs 278 people. Type your own figures above.

3The formula

A survey does not need to ask everyone — it needs enough people that the result is close to the truth. The margin of error is how close, and the confidence level is how sure:

Sample size — in words: work out the size for an endless population from the z-value and the margin of error, assuming half the people answer each way, then shrink it with the finite population correction. n0 = z² × 0.25 ÷ e², then n = n0 ÷ (1 + (n0 − 1) ÷ population), rounded up

Formula source: Cochran's sample size formula with the finite population correction, using z-values 1.645 for 90%, 1.96 for 95% and 2.576 for 99%, and a share of 0.5, the most careful case. No outside data is used — only the numbers you type.

4Worked example: population 1,000, 95% confidence, 5% margin of error

  1. Start with the figures: population 1,000, 95% confidence (z = 1.96), margin of error 5.00%.
  2. Work out the size for an endless population: 1.96² × 0.25 ÷ 0.05² = 384.16.
  3. Allow for the population: 384.16 ÷ (1 + (384.16 − 1) ÷ 1,000) = 384.16 ÷ 1.38316 = 277.74.
  4. Round up to a whole person: 278.
  5. Answer: survey 278 people.

Try it in the tool above: type 1000, pick 95% and type 5, and the result line shows 278.

6Questions about sample size

What does the margin of error mean?

It is how far the survey result may sit from the true value. A margin of error of 5% means that if the survey says 60% of people agree, the true figure is likely between 55% and 65%. A smaller margin needs a bigger sample.

What does the confidence level mean?

It is how sure you want to be. At 95% confidence, about 95 out of 100 surveys done the same way would land inside the margin of error. Higher confidence needs a bigger sample: 99% needs more people than 95% for the same margin.

Why does the formula assume half the people answer each way?

Because a 50-50 split is the hardest case to measure, so it needs the biggest sample. If the true split is 90-10, fewer people would be enough, but the split is not known before the survey, so the calculator plans for the careful case.

What if the population is very large?

Then the population hardly matters: the size settles near the endless-population figure. For 95% confidence and a 5% margin of error, that figure is 385 people, whatever the population is above about a million.

What if the population is small?

Then the correction matters a lot: a small population needs a smaller sample, because a bigger share of it is asked. For example, a population of 500 with 90% confidence and a 5% margin needs only 176 people. The population must be at least 10.
