2Quick answer

With A in a 3-credit course, B in a 3-credit course and A in a 4-credit course, the GPA is 3.70. Each grade is worth points — A is 4, B is 3, C is 2, D is 1 and F is 0 — and the GPA is total grade points divided by total credits. More credits means more weight.

3The formula

GPA is a weighted average on the 4.0 scale. The weight is the credit hours, so a 4-credit course counts more than a 3-credit course:

GPA — in words: turn each grade into points, multiply by the course credits, add them up, and divide by the total credits. GPA = (grade points × credits, added over all courses) ÷ total credits; A = 4, B = 3, C = 2, D = 1, F = 0

Formula source: the standard 4.0 GPA scale used by schools and colleges in the USA and Canada. No data is fetched from anywhere — you type your own grades and credits, and the page uses only those numbers.

4Worked example: A (3 credits), B (3 credits), A (4 credits)

  1. Start with the courses: A in 3 credits, B in 3 credits, A in 4 credits.
  2. Turn grades into points: A is 4, B is 3. Course points are 4 × 3 = 12, 3 × 3 = 9, and 4 × 4 = 16.
  3. Add the points: 12 + 9 + 16 = 37. Add the credits: 3 + 3 + 4 = 10.
  4. Answer: GPA = 37 ÷ 10 = 3.70.

Try it in the tool above: choose A, B and A with 3, 3 and 4 credits, and the result line shows 3.70.

6Questions about gpa grade

What do the letters mean in points?

On the 4.0 scale, A is worth 4 points, B is 3, C is 2, D is 1 and F is 0. Some schools add plus and minus grades, like A− at 3.7 — this calculator uses the plain letters only.

Why do credits matter?

Because the GPA is weighted by credits. An A in a 4-credit course pulls the GPA up more than an A in a 1-credit course, and an F in a 4-credit course pulls it down more.

What is a good GPA?

On the 4.0 scale, 3.5 and above is usually strong, 3.0 is a solid B average, and below 2.0 can put you on probation at many schools. What counts as good depends on your school and your goal.

What if I have more than four courses?

Work out the GPA for four courses at a time, then combine the results: add all the grade points together and divide by all the credits together. Or run the calculator again with the other courses and combine the two totals by hand.

Does a failed course count?

Yes. An F is worth 0 points but its credits still count in the total, so it pulls the GPA down. Leaving the course out would give a wrong, higher GPA.

Is this an official GPA?

No. This is a plain weighted average from the grades and credits you type. Your school may count plus and minus grades, repeated courses or transfer credits differently — check its own rules.
