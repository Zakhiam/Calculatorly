2Quick answer

A confidence interval is the sample mean plus or minus a margin of error, where the margin is the z-value for the confidence level times the standard error. For example, a mean of 50 with a standard deviation of 5 and a sample of 25 gives a 95% interval of 48.0400 to 51.9600. Type your own figures above.

3The formula

A sample mean is only an estimate of the true mean, so the interval shows the range the true mean is likely to sit in. A higher confidence level gives a wider range:

Confidence interval — in words: divide the standard deviation by the square root of the sample size to get the standard error, multiply it by the z-value for the confidence level to get the margin of error, then add and subtract that margin from the mean. interval = mean ± z × (standard deviation ÷ √sample size)

Formula source: the standard normal confidence interval for a mean, with z-values 1.645 for 90%, 1.96 for 95% and 2.576 for 99%. No outside data is used — only the numbers you type.

4Worked example: mean 50, standard deviation 5, sample 25, 95% confidence

  1. Start with the figures: sample mean 50, standard deviation 5, sample size 25, 95% confidence.
  2. Find the standard error: 5 ÷ √25 = 5 ÷ 5 = 1.0000.
  3. Find the margin of error: 1.96 × 1.0000 = 1.9600, using the z-value for 95% confidence.
  4. Add and subtract the margin from the mean: 50 − 1.9600 = 48.0400, and 50 + 1.9600 = 51.9600.
  5. Answer: the 95% confidence interval is 48.0400 to 51.9600.

Try it in the tool above: type 50, 5 and 25, pick 95%, and the result line shows 48.0400 to 51.9600.

6Questions about confidence interval

What does a 95% confidence interval mean?

It means that if the same sampling were repeated many times, about 95 out of 100 of the intervals worked out that way would hold the true mean. It does not mean there is a 95% chance that this one interval holds it — the true mean is fixed, and the interval is the estimate.

Why does a higher confidence level give a wider interval?

Because being more sure needs more room. The z-value grows with the confidence level — 1.645 for 90%, 1.96 for 95% and 2.576 for 99% — so the margin of error grows with it, and the interval widens. A 99% interval is wider than a 95% interval from the same sample.

Why does a bigger sample give a narrower interval?

Because the standard error is the standard deviation divided by the square root of the sample size. A bigger sample divides by a bigger number, so the standard error and the margin of error shrink, and the interval narrows.

What if the sample size is 1?

The interval cannot be worked out from a sample of one: a single value gives no sense of the spread, and the formula needs a sample size of at least 2. The calculator shows a clear message instead of a made-up number.

Does this assume the data follows a normal shape?

Yes. This interval uses the normal z-values, which suits a large sample or data that is roughly bell-shaped. For a small sample from data that is far from bell-shaped, the interval is only a rough guide — a statistics textbook or a teacher can advise on the right method.
