2Quick answer

A logarithm to a base is the power that base must be raised to in order to give the number. Log base 10 of 100 is 2.0000000000, because 10 raised to the power 2 is 100. Log base 2 of 8 is 3.0000000000, because 2 raised to the power 3 is 8. Type your own number and base above.

3The formula

A log answers the question “to what power must I raise the base to get this number?” Any base works, as long as it is above zero and not equal to 1:

Logarithm of any base — in words: divide the natural log of the number by the natural log of the base. log base b of x = ln(x) ÷ ln(b)

Formula source: the standard change-of-base rule for logarithms. No outside data is used — only the two numbers you type.

6Questions about log

What is a logarithm in plain words?

It is the power you must raise a base to in order to get a number. Log base 10 of 100 is 2, because 10² = 100. Log base 2 of 8 is 3, because 2³ = 8. The log undoes a power, just as division undoes multiplication.

What is the difference between log base 10, natural log and log base 2?

Only the base changes. Log base 10 is the common log, used for scales like decibels. The natural log uses base e, about 2.718281828, and is used in growth and decay. Log base 2 is used in computing, because computers work in binary. This calculator does all three — just type the base.

Why must the number be above zero?

Because no power of a positive base can give zero or a negative number: a base raised to any power stays positive. So the log of zero or a negative number is not a real number, and the calculator shows a clear message instead of a made-up number.

Why can the base not be 1?

Because 1 raised to any power is always 1, so it can never give any other number — the question “1 to what power gives 100?” has no answer. The base must also be above zero. The calculator shows a clear message for a base of 1 or below zero.

How do I get a natural log here?

Type 2.718281828 as the base. For example, the natural log of 10 is 2.3025850934. The table under the result also shows the natural log of your number and of your base, so you can see the working.
