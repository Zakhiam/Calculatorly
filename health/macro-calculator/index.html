2Quick answer

On 2,000 calories a day split 30% protein, 40% carbs and 30% fat, you eat 150 g of protein, 200 g of carbs and 67 g of fat. Protein and carbs give 4 calories per gram, fat gives 9. The three percents must add up to 100, or the calculator shows a clear message instead of a made-up answer.

3The formula

Each gram of protein or carbs gives 4 calories, and each gram of fat gives 9 calories. Working backwards from calories gives grams:

Macros — in words: take the calories for each macro, divide protein and carbs by 4 and fat by 9, to get grams. protein grams = calories × protein% ÷ 100 ÷ 4; carb grams = calories × carb% ÷ 100 ÷ 4; fat grams = calories × fat% ÷ 100 ÷ 9

Formula source: the standard energy values of 4 calories per gram for protein and carbs and 9 calories per gram for fat. No data is fetched from anywhere — you type your own calories and split, and the page uses only those four numbers.

4Worked example: 2,000 calories, 30/40/30

  1. Start with the numbers: 2,000 calories a day, split 30% protein, 40% carbs, 30% fat.
  2. Protein calories are 2,000 × 30 ÷ 100 = 600, and 600 ÷ 4 = 150 g.
  3. Carb calories are 2,000 × 40 ÷ 100 = 800, and 800 ÷ 4 = 200 g. Fat calories are 600, and 600 ÷ 9 = 67 g.
  4. Answer: protein 150 g, carbs 200 g, fat 67 g.

Try it in the tool above: type 2000, 30, 40 and 30, and the result line shows the three amounts in grams.

6Questions about macro

Why must the percents add up to 100?

Because the three macros together make up all of your calories. If they add up to 90 or 110, some calories are missing or counted twice, so no honest answer exists. The calculator shows a clear message until the split adds up to 100.

Why does fat give fewer grams than protein for the same calories?

Because fat is more energy-dense: 9 calories per gram against 4 for protein and carbs. The same 600 calories is 150 g of protein but only 67 g of fat.

What split should I use?

A common starting split is 30% protein, 40% carbs and 30% fat. People who train hard often raise protein, and people who eat low-carb raise fat. Your own goal and your doctor's advice matter more than any one split.

Where do my daily calories come from?

From your total daily energy use — your base rate plus activity. Use the calorie tool on this site to work that out first, then type the number here.

Are the grams rounded?

Yes, to whole numbers, half-up. For example, 66.67 g of fat rounds to 67 g. The calories in the table use the same rounded grams, so the figures agree.

Is this medical or diet advice?

No. This is a general guide only, worked out from the numbers you type. It is not a diet plan and never replaces advice from a doctor or a dietitian.
