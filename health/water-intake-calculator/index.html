2Quick answer

A common guide is 35 millilitres of water per kilogram of body weight each day, plus 400 millilitres for each active hour. At 70 kilograms with 1 active hour, that is 2.9 litres a day. Type your weight and activity above and this calculator shows your daily amount.

3The formula

This is a general guide for a healthy adult in a mild climate. Hot weather, illness, pregnancy and breastfeeding all raise the amount, and food also gives water.

Daily water intake — in words: multiply the body weight in kilograms by 35 millilitres, add 400 millilitres for each active hour, then divide by 1,000 to get litres. litres a day = (weight in kg × 35 + active hours × 400) ÷ 1,000

Formula source: the common 35 ml per kilogram guide plus 400 ml per active hour, on the two numbers you type. This is a general guide only, not medical advice.

4Worked example: 70 kg, 1 active hour

  1. Base amount: 70 × 35 = 2,450 millilitres.
  2. Extra for activity: 1 × 400 = 400 millilitres.
  3. Total: 2,450 + 400 = 2,850 millilitres, or 2.85 litres.
  4. Answer: 2.9 litres a day, rounded half-up to 1 decimal place.

Try it in the tool above: type 70 and 1, and the result line shows 2.9 litres.

6Questions about water intake

Does food count towards water intake?

Yes. About a fifth of daily water usually comes from food, especially fruit, vegetables and soups. This calculator gives the total water to aim for, from drinks and food together.

Do I need more water in hot weather?

Yes. Heat and sweating raise the amount you lose, so drink more than the figure shown here. Thirst is a late sign — drink before you feel very thirsty.

Can I drink too much water?

Yes, though it is rare. Drinking far more than the body needs can dilute the blood. If you drink much more than the figure here every day, ask a doctor.

What if I weigh less than 20 kg or more than 300 kg?

This page accepts weights from 20 to 300 kilograms. Outside that range it shows a message instead of a result, because the guide is not made for those weights.

Is this medical advice?

No. This is a general guide only, worked out from the numbers you type. It never replaces advice from a doctor or another health professional.

Is this calculator free, and do I need to sign up?

Yes, it is free, and no sign-up is needed. Nothing is uploaded: the calculation runs in your browser on your own device, and the numbers you type are not sent anywhere.
