2Quick answer

Running 10 km in 50 minutes is a pace of 5:00 min/km, which is 8:03 min/mile. The pace per kilometre is the time divided by the distance, and the pace per mile is the same time over the distance in miles. Your speed is 12.00 km/h, or 7.46 miles per hour.

3The formula

Pace is time per unit of distance. One mile is 1.60934 kilometres, so the mile pace is always the kilometre pace times 1.60934:

Running pace — in words: divide the time in minutes by the distance, for the pace per kilometre; multiply by 1.60934 for the pace per mile. pace per km = time in minutes ÷ distance in km; pace per mile = pace per km × 1.60934; speed = distance ÷ time in hours

Formula source: the standard definitions of pace and speed, with 1 mile = 1.60934 km. No data is fetched from anywhere — you type your own distance and time, and the page uses only those two numbers.

4Worked example: 10 km in 50 minutes

  1. Start with the numbers: distance 10 km, time 50 minutes.
  2. Pace per kilometre: 50 ÷ 10 = 5.00 minutes, which is 5:00 min/km.
  3. Pace per mile: 5.00 × 1.60934 = 8.05 minutes, which is 8:03 min/mile (0.05 minutes is 3 seconds).
  4. Answer: 5:00 min/km, 8:03 min/mile. Speed is 10 km ÷ (50 ÷ 60) hours = 12.00 km/h.

Try it in the tool above: type 10 and 50, and the result line shows 5:00 min/km, 8:03 min/mile.

6Questions about pace

How do I type a time with seconds?

Type the time as minutes with a decimal: 50 minutes 30 seconds is 50.5 minutes. The pace is shown in minutes and seconds, rounded to the nearest second.

Why is the mile pace slower than the kilometre pace?

Because a mile is longer than a kilometre — 1.60934 km. It takes longer to cover a mile than a kilometre at the same speed, so the minutes per mile are always higher.

What is a good pace?

It depends on the distance and on you. A 5:00 min/km pace over 10 km is strong for a regular runner; over a marathon it is very fast. Compare your pace with your own past runs, not with strangers.

Can I use miles for the distance?

This page takes kilometres. To change miles to kilometres, multiply the miles by 1.60934 — for example, 6.2 miles is 10 km.

What if the distance is zero?

A pace over zero distance has no answer, because the sum divides by the distance. The calculator shows a clear message instead of a made-up number.

Is this medical advice?

No. This is a plain time-and-distance sum. Build up distance slowly, and ask a doctor before starting hard training if you have a health condition.
