2Quick answer

Your basal metabolic rate, or BMR, is the calories your body burns at complete rest, just to keep you alive. For example, a 30-year-old man of 70 kg and 175 cm burns about 1,649 calories a day at rest. Type your weight, height, age and sex above and this calculator gives your BMR instantly.

3The formula

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is the most widely used way to estimate resting burn from body measurements:

BMR — in words: take ten times your weight in kilograms, add six and a quarter times your height in centimetres, subtract five times your age, then add five for men or subtract 161 for women. BMR = 10 × weight + 6.25 × height − 5 × age + 5 for men, or − 161 for women

Formula source: the Mifflin-St Jeor equation (1990) for basal metabolic rate. This is a guide only, not medical advice.

4Worked example: man, 30 years, 70 kg, 175 cm

  1. Start with the figures: weight 70 kg, height 175 cm, age 30, male.
  2. Work out the parts: 10 × 70 = 700; 6.25 × 175 = 1,093.75; 5 × 30 = 150.
  3. Put them together: 700 + 1,093.75 − 150 + 5 = 1,648.75, which rounds to 1,649.
  4. Answer: the BMR is about 1,649 calories a day at complete rest.

Try it in the tool above: type 70, 175 and 30 with Male picked, and the result line shows 1,649.

6Questions about bmr

What is BMR?

Basal Metabolic Rate: the calories your body burns at complete rest, just to stay alive — breathing, keeping warm and running your organs. It does not include moving around, exercise or digesting food.

Is BMR the same as my daily calorie need?

No. Your daily need, or TDEE, is your BMR times an activity factor, because moving around and exercise burn more on top. The table under your result shows your daily need at three activity levels, and the TDEE calculator on this site works it out in full.

Why is BMR lower for women?

On average, women carry less muscle and more body fat than men of the same weight and height, and muscle burns more calories at rest than fat. The equation allows for that with a different constant: plus 5 for men, minus 161 for women.

Does BMR change with age?

Yes, it falls slowly as you get older, because muscle is lost and the body uses less energy at rest. The equation subtracts 5 calories a day for every year of age.

Can I raise my BMR?

Building muscle raises it a little, because muscle burns more calories at rest than fat. But the change is small next to the calories burned by moving and exercising, so activity matters far more than BMR alone.

How are the answers rounded?

Every result on this page uses one rounding rule: whole numbers, no decimal places. The BMR is rounded to a whole calorie, and the daily needs in the table are that rounded BMR times each activity factor, rounded to whole calories, so the figures always agree.
