2Quick answer

A $100 bill with a 10% discount, 8% tax and a 15% tip comes to $111.78 in total. The discount comes off first, leaving $90.00, then tax of $7.20 takes it to $97.20, then a tip of $14.58 on that total gives $111.78. The order matters, because tax and tip are worked out on the discounted price.

3The formula

The three steps run in a fixed order — discount first, then tax, then tip — because tax and tip are worked out on the price after discount:

Tip, tax and discount — in words: take the discount off the price, add tax on what is left, then add the tip on the taxed total. discounted price = price × (1 − discount% ÷ 100); tax = discounted price × tax% ÷ 100; tip = (discounted price + tax) × tip% ÷ 100; total = discounted price + tax + tip

Formula source: the standard arithmetic of percent off, percent tax and percent tip, in the order discount, tax, tip. No rate is fetched from anywhere — you type your own percents, and the page uses only the four numbers you type.

4Worked example: $100 with 10% off, 8% tax, 15% tip

  1. Start with the numbers: price $100, discount 10%, tax 8%, tip 15%.
  2. Discount first: $100 × (1 − 10 ÷ 100) = $90.00. The saving is $10.00.
  3. Tax on the discounted price: $90.00 × 8 ÷ 100 = $7.20, so the total is $97.20. Tip on that total: $97.20 × 15 ÷ 100 = $14.58.
  4. Answer: the final amount is $97.20 + $14.58 = $111.78.

Try it in the tool above: type 100, 10, 8 and 15, and the result line shows $111.78.

6Questions about tip tax discount

Why does the order matter?

Because tax and tip are worked out on the price after discount. If the tip were worked out on the full $100 instead of $97.20, it would be $15.00 instead of $14.58, and the total would be $112.20 — a different answer.

Should the tip be on the price before or after tax?

This calculator puts the tip on the price after tax, which is how many people tip in the USA. Some people tip on the price before tax instead — that gives a smaller tip, and a smaller total.

What if there is no discount, tax or tip?

Put 0 for that step and it is skipped. With 0% discount, 0% tax and 0% tip, the total is just the price you typed.

Can the discount be more than 100%?

No sensible discount is over 100% — that would make the price negative. If you type a discount over 100, the calculator shows a clear message instead of a made-up number.

Does this split the bill between people?

No. This gives the final total for one bill. To split it, use the tip and bill split tool on this site, which divides a total between people.

Is this financial advice?

No. This is a plain percent sum for planning, from the numbers you type. Tax rules differ by place, so check the rate where you are.
