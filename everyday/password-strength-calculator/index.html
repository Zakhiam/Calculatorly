3The formula

Lowercase letters add 26, uppercase letters add 26, digits add 10, and symbols add 33 to the character set. Under 28 bits is Very weak, under 36 is Weak, under 60 is Fair, under 80 is Strong, and 80 or more is Very strong.

Password strength — in words: work out how many different characters the password could use, then multiply the length by the log of that number to get bits of entropy. entropy in bits = password length × log2(size of the character set)

Formula source: Shannon entropy on the character classes in the password you type, with the crack time worked out at 10 billion guesses a second. The password never leaves your browser. This checks length and character mix only, not dictionary words.

4Worked example: Tr0ub4dor&3

  1. Length: 11 characters. Character set: lowercase, uppercase, digits and symbols, so 26 + 26 + 10 + 33 = 95.
  2. Entropy: 11 × log2(95) = 72 bits, rounded to a whole number.
  3. Band: 72 bits is under 80, so the strength is Strong.
  4. Answer: Strong (72 bits). At 10 billion guesses a second, trying half the combinations would take about 9,000 years.

Try it in the tool above: type Tr0ub4dor&3, and the result line shows Strong (72 bits).

6Questions about password strength

Is my password sent anywhere?

No. The password is checked only in your browser on your own device. It is never sent, stored or logged anywhere, and this page has no way to see it.

What do the bits mean?

Bits of entropy measure how many guesses an attacker must try. Each extra bit doubles the guesses needed. A 72-bit password needs about 9,000 years at 10 billion guesses a second, while a 38-bit password needs about 10 seconds.

Why is the word password only Fair?

Because it is short and uses lowercase letters only: 8 letters from 26 gives 38 bits. It is also a common word, which this entropy check cannot see — a real attacker tries common words first, so it is weaker in practice than the bits suggest.

What makes a password strong?

Length matters most. A long phrase of random words beats a short password with symbols. Aim for at least 80 bits: for example, 14 characters mixing all four character types, or 20 lowercase letters picked at random.

Should I reuse a strong password?

No. A strong password used on many sites is still one leak away from all of them. Use a different password for each important account, and a password manager to keep them.

Is this calculator free, and do I need to sign up?

Yes, it is free, and no sign-up is needed. Nothing is uploaded: the check runs in your browser on your own device.
