2Quick answer

Pack A costs $3.00 for 500 units, which is $0.0060 per unit, while Pack B costs $5.00 for 1,000 units, which is $0.0050 per unit. Pack B is the better value, because its price per unit is lower. The pack with the lower price per unit always wins, even when its total price is higher.

3The formula

Pack prices mislead, because packs hold different amounts. Dividing by the size gives the true cost of one unit:

Unit price — in words: divide each pack's price by its size, then compare the two prices per unit. unit price = pack price ÷ pack size; better value = the pack with the lower unit price

Formula source: the standard definition of unit price. No data is fetched from anywhere — you type your own prices and sizes, and the page uses only those four numbers.

4Worked example: $3.00 for 500 g against $5.00 for 1,000 g

  1. Start with the numbers: Pack A is $3.00 for 500, Pack B is $5.00 for 1,000, both in the same unit.
  2. Pack A unit price: $3.00 ÷ 500 = $0.0060 per unit.
  3. Pack B unit price: $5.00 ÷ 1,000 = $0.0050 per unit.
  4. Answer: Pack B is better value, at $0.0050 per unit against $0.0060 — a saving of $0.0010 per unit.

Try it in the tool above: type 3 and 500 for Pack A and 5 and 1000 for Pack B, and the result line shows Pack B.

6Questions about unit price

Why is the dearer pack sometimes better value?

Because it holds more. A $5.00 pack of 1,000 units costs $0.0050 per unit, while a $3.00 pack of 500 costs $0.0060 per unit — so the dearer pack is cheaper per unit, and buying two small packs would cost $6.00.

Must both sizes be in the same unit?

Yes. Comparing 500 grams against 1 litre is meaningless — the units must match. Use both in grams, both in ml, or both as a count of items.

What if the unit prices are exactly the same?

Then neither pack wins, and the calculator says both packs cost the same. Pick the pack size that suits you — the smaller one if you use little, the larger one if you use a lot.

What if a pack size is zero?

A size of zero has no answer, because the sum divides by the size. The calculator shows a clear message instead of a made-up number.

Does this count quality?

No. It counts price per unit only. A cheaper pack of poor quality can still be the worse buy — check the product, not just the price.

Is this financial advice?

No. This is a plain division for planning, from the numbers you type.
